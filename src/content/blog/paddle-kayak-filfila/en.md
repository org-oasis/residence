<p>Yes, <strong>paddleboarding and kayaking</strong> are easy to practise along Filfila's beaches, 700 m from Résidence Oasis, in water that is generally sheltered and reaches around 26°C in August. The bathing season runs from June to November, leaving a wide window to get started or practise more regularly on this stretch of the Skikda coastline.</p>

<p>For a traveller planning an active stay in Filfila, paddleboarding and kayaking often remain a last-minute discovery rather than a reason to travel in themselves, alongside classic swimming. This article details what these two activities actually offer on the Skikda coast, the practical differences between them, where and how to rent gear, precautions to know as a family, and the best way to combine them with the rest of a programme built around Filfila.</p>

<h2>Paddleboarding and kayaking in Filfila, an activity accessible straight from the beach</h2>

<p>Unlike jet-skiing or scuba diving, <strong>paddleboarding</strong> and <strong>kayaking</strong> require no certification or mandatory supervision for a first outing in calm water. It's one of the simplest water activities to fit into a classic beach day in Filfila, with no organisation needed beyond renting gear on site.</p>

<h3>Sheltered water ideal for beginners</h3>

<p>The beaches of the Jeanne d'Arc district and the nearby coves of Filfila offer areas relatively sheltered from the prevailing wind, favouring flat water suited to a first paddleboard or kayak attempt. This setup noticeably lowers the difficulty for a beginner compared with an outing on more open sea, where chop makes balance more demanding.</p>

<h2>Paddleboard or kayak: which to choose depending on level</h2>

<p>The choice between the two mainly depends on the outing's purpose and each person's comfort level at sea. Neither requires prior experience, but they differ noticeably once on the water.</p>

<h3>Paddleboarding: balance and versatility</h3>

<p>A <strong>paddleboard</strong> (or stand-up paddle) is used standing on a board with a single paddle. The first minutes involve finding your balance, generally easier seated or kneeling before standing up. Once mastered, paddleboarding lets you follow the coast at your own pace, stop to swim wherever you like, and suits a short, solo outing well.</p>

<h3>Kayaking: more stable for a longer outing</h3>

<p>A <strong>kayak</strong> is used seated, giving immediate stability and a more even effort spread across the arms and torso. It lets you cover a longer distance without excessive fatigue, particularly on a tandem model that suits a parent with a child or a couple well. It's generally the more reassuring option for a first family outing.</p>

<h2>Where to paddleboard and kayak around Filfila</h2>

<p>Two options stand out depending on the time available and the wish to explore further than the usual beach.</p>

<h3>The sheltered beaches of the Jeanne d'Arc district</h3>

<p>For a first outing or a short session, the beaches near the residence are largely enough. They let you practise without a long trip, return easily to the starting point if tired, and keep an eye on the rest of the group left on the sand.</p>

<h3>Toward Stora, for a longer outing</h3>

<p>For more confident practitioners, following the coast toward <a href="/en/blog/plage-stora">Stora beach</a> offers a different setting, between rocky coves and the approach to the fishing harbour. This stretch of coastline demands more experience and favourable sea conditions; it suits a kayak better than a paddleboard given the distance involved.</p>

<h2>Paddleboarding and kayaking with children</h2>

<p>A <strong>tandem kayak</strong> remains the most suitable option for an outing with young children, provided you stay in a sheltered area and use a properly fitted life jacket. Standing paddleboarding, more demanding on balance, suits an older child better, under an adult's constant supervision nearby.</p>

<ul>
  <li>Always wear a properly fitted life jacket, even for an adult comfortable at sea</li>
  <li>Stay within the usual swimming area rather than heading off alone</li>
  <li>Plan for extra sun protection, exposure on the water being stronger than on the sand</li>
  <li>Check wind and sea conditions before setting out, especially outside the summer season</li>
  <li>Pack quick-drying clothing and water shoes for launching</li>
</ul>

<h2>When to paddleboard and kayak: the best time</h2>

<p>Early morning generally remains the most comfortable time, the sea often being flatter before the wind picks up during the day. The bathing season officially runs from June to November in Skikda, with water reaching around 26°C in August. July and August bring the highest beach turnout and therefore the widest gear supply, but also the longest waits to rent. June, September and early October offer a good compromise, with a calmer sea and more availability from rental operators.</p>

<h2>Common mistakes to avoid</h2>

<ul>
  <li>Heading off alone on a paddleboard or kayak beyond the usually frequented areas</li>
  <li>Setting out without checking wind conditions, a standing paddleboard being more sensitive to gusts than a kayak</li>
