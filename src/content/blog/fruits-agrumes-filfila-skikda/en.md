<p>The stalls of Filfila and Skikda city centre, <strong>20 km</strong> away, offer a wide range of fruit depending on the season: citrus (oranges, clementines, lemons) from November to February, then figs, grapes, and watermelon from June to September. Prices stay noticeably lower than in Europe.</p>

<p>For a traveller planning a stay in Filfila, whether a family looking for healthy produce for the children or a couple curious about local cooking, knowing which fruit to look for and when changes the experience of shopping on site. This article details the fruit and citrus calendar in the Skikda region, the best places to find them near Résidence Oasis, and how to easily work them into a stay in an equipped apartment.</p>

<h2>The fruit and citrus calendar in the Skikda region</h2>

<p>As along the rest of the Algerian Mediterranean coast, fruit production in Skikda follows a marked seasonal calendar, with two clearly distinct periods for the visitor: the winter of citrus and the summer of water-rich fruit.</p>

<h3>Winter, citrus season</h3>

<p>From November to February, oranges, clementines, and lemons dominate the region's stalls. This period coincides with the low tourist season on the coast, which often makes it possible to combine a more affordable stay with citrus at its freshest. The mild, damp winter climate of the Skikda area, already mentioned for coastal walks or quiet stays, also suits citrus growing well in the nearby hinterland.</p>

<h3>Summer, season of refreshing fruit</h3>

<p>From June to September, watermelon and melon take over the stalls, joined by figs in late summer and grapes depending on the year. These fruits naturally go with swimming season, appreciated for their freshness after a day at the beach in <a href="/en/blog/poisson-frais-stora">Stora</a> or Filfila.</p>

<h3>Fruit available all year round</h3>

<p>Apples, bananas, and some imported citrus round out the offer year-round at the markets, alongside local seasonal production. For an extended stay, mixing local seasonal produce with fruit available continuously remains the simplest approach, especially for a family wanting balanced meals without relying solely on restaurants.</p>

<p>Algerian diaspora visitors often find on these stalls the same varieties they knew growing up, sometimes tasting more authentic than abroad, since the fruit is sold close to where it's grown and without long-distance transport.</p>

<h3>The convenience shops of the Jeanne d'Arc district</h3>

<p>For a quick top-up or small quantities, the convenience shops of the Jeanne d'Arc district in Filfila are more than enough, just a few minutes' walk from the residence, 700 m from the sea.</p>

<h3>Skikda city centre's traditional market</h3>

<p>For a wider choice and generally better prices on larger quantities, the traditional market in Skikda city centre, about 20 km from Filfila, remains the province's reference point. A round trip by car or taxi lets you combine this errand with another visit to the city centre the same day.</p>

<h2>What to do with leftover fruit during a short stay</h2>

<p>Purchases at Skikda city centre's traditional market are often made in larger quantities than at a convenience shop, which raises the question of leftovers for a stay of just a few days.</p>

<h3>Easy homemade jam and juice</h3>

<p>Surplus citrus turns easily into fresh-pressed morning juice, while a simple orange or clementine jam can be made on the apartment's stovetop in under an hour, with just the fruit and sugar. It's a pleasant activity to share as a family during an off-season stay, when more days are spent indoors.</p>

<h3>Washing thoroughly before eating</h3>

<p>As with any fruit and vegetables bought at an open-air market, washing thoroughly before eating remains a basic precaution, particularly for fruit eaten with the skin on. The apartment's fridge lets you keep washed fruit fresh for longer, especially useful during the hottest summer months.</p>

<h2>Practical tips for buying fruit during the stay</h2>

<ul>
  <li>Compare a few stalls before buying in bulk, as prices can vary from one vendor to another</li>
  <li>Favour seasonal fruit over out-of-season produce, which is pricier and often less flavourful</li>
  <li>Buy in small, regular amounts rather than all at once for a multi-day stay without a large fridge</li>
  <li>Bring a reusable bag, as not all convenience shops systematically provide plastic bags</li>
  <li>Ask locally for tips on spotting ripe fruit, as visual cues vary by local variety</li>
</ul>
