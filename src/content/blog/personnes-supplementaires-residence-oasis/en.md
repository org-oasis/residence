<p>The question comes up often with families traveling with grandparents, teenagers, or friends, who don't fit exactly into the advertised capacity of a Studio, an F2, or an F3. Rather than enforcing a single fixed format or refusing any flexibility, Résidence Oasis applies one simple rule, identical across its 7 units: a capped number of extra guests, for a per-night, per-person surcharge. <h2>How many extra guests can the residence host</h2>

<p>The rule applied across all 7 apartments at the residence is simple and deliberately identical from one unit type to another, to avoid any ambiguity when booking.</p>

<h3>The rule: up to 2 extra guests, per apartment</h3>

<p>Every apartment at Résidence Oasis can host up to <strong>2 extra guests</strong> beyond its base capacity, whether it's a Studio built for 3, an F2 for 4, or an F3 for 8. This cap does not vary by unit type: it is a flexibility margin, not a doubled capacity.</p>

<h3>A surcharge per guest, not per apartment</h3>

<p>The <strong>€{{EXTRA_PERSON_EUR}}/night ({{EXTRA_PERSON_DZD}} DA)</strong> surcharge is calculated per added guest, not as a flat fee per apartment. Adding one extra guest costs that single amount per night; adding two doubles the surcharge, still per night.</p>

<h2>How the extra-guest surcharge actually works</h2>

<h3>A fixed amount per night and per guest</h3>

<p>The €{{EXTRA_PERSON_EUR}}/night ({{EXTRA_PERSON_DZD}} DA) surcharge per guest applies for every night of the stay, not as a one-off amount on arrival. For a one-week stay with one extra guest, the amount is therefore multiplied by the number of nights booked.</p>

<h3>Settled with the balance, on arrival</h3>

<p>As with the rest of the stay, the one-night deposit is paid by CCP bank transfer when booking, and the balance, including the extra-guest surcharge, is paid on arrival in dinars or euros, with no deposit required. It remains best to state the exact number of extra guests as soon as you make contact, so the total amount is clear before arrival.</p>

<h2>Why this 2-guest cap exists</h2>

<p>The cap of 2 extra guests per apartment is not arbitrary: it matches the margin each unit can absorb without hurting the base comfort level. A 3-person Studio raised to 5, or a 4-person F2 raised to 6, keeps a reasonable amount of extra sleeping space without turning the living room into a permanent dormitory. Beyond that threshold, the quality of the stay would decline for every occupant, whatever the unit type booked.</p>

<p>The same cap also applies to the F3 with terrace, even though it is noticeably larger than the other unit types. The reasoning stays the same: the 8 guests included in the base capacity already fill the main sleeping spaces, and the 2 extra guests correspond to the reasonable hosting margin available, not an unlimited extension of capacity.</p>

<h2>Anticipating extra guests based on the time of year</h2>

<p>The number of extra guests to plan for often depends on the chosen period. In high season, from July to August, extended families and groups of friends typically use school holidays to gather across several generations, which makes the extra-guest option particularly useful during that period. Off-season, between October and May, stays are more often limited to a unit's base capacity, as occasional visits from relatives are less frequent.</p>

<p>For a stay of 7 nights or more with extra guests, the 10% discount applied to the base nightly rate makes the overall cost more accessible, even though the per-guest surcharge is still calculated separately for each night of the stay. It remains advisable to state the final number of travelers at booking time rather than during the stay, so the host can arrange bedding and linens accordingly.</p>

<h2>Booking several apartments rather than exceeding the cap</h2>

<p>For a group of more than 10, or one that exceeds a single unit's maximum capacity even with the 2 extra guests, the solution is to book several of the residence's 7 apartments in parallel rather than trying to exceed the set cap.
