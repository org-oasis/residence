<p>Yes, <strong>snorkeling works well in Filfila</strong>, less than 1 km from Résidence Oasis: the rocky points framing Larbi Ben M'hidi beach shelter sea urchins, small fish and crabs visible from just 1 to 2 m deep. The most favorable season runs from June to November, with water around 26 °C in August, ideal for a first session without a wetsuit.</p>

<p>Unlike scuba diving, which requires training and professional supervision, <strong>snorkeling (or free diving)</strong> stays accessible to the whole family with lightweight gear: mask, snorkel, fins. The trick is knowing where to go along the Skikda coastline to find clear water and interesting rocky terrain rather than a flat, featureless stretch of sand. This article covers the best spots around Filfila, the gear to bring, the ideal season, and the precautions worth knowing before getting started.</p>

<h2>The best snorkeling spots around Filfila</h2>

<p>The Filfila coastline alternates between sandy beaches and rocky points, the latter being the most interesting for observing marine life at the surface. The purely sandy stretches, great for everyday family swimming, offer little interest for snorkeling for lack of underwater relief.</p>

<h3>The rocky points of Larbi Ben M'hidi</h3>

<p>At the edges of <a href="/en/blog/plage-larbi-ben-mhidi">Larbi Ben M'hidi</a> beach, the rocky outcrops create small coves where the water stays calm while offering varied terrain: rocks, crevices, small schools of fish. This is the most convenient area from Résidence Oasis, reachable on foot in a few minutes.</p>

<h3>The coves toward Stora</h3>

<p>For a wilder outing, the rocky coastline heading toward Stora holds less crowded coves, often with clearer water. Access is less direct and usually requires a car or taxi, but the quiet and visibility largely make up for the detour.</p>

<h3>What not to expect in Filfila</h3>

<p>There is no coral reef or accessible wreck for snorkeling in Filfila: the marine life you will see is typical of a rocky Mediterranean coastline (sea urchins, small fish, sometimes starfish or small octopuses). Expectations should stay realistic compared with a tropical destination.</p>

<h3>Water shoes: the often-forgotten accessory</h3>

<p>On Filfila's rocky areas, walking barefoot to the water risks cuts from shells or sea urchins. Lightweight water shoes avoid this discomfort and make it easier to get into the water from the rocks rather than from sand.</p>

<h2>When to go snorkeling in Skikda</h2>

<p>The <strong>swimming season</strong> in Skikda runs roughly from June to November. Each period has different advantages for snorkeling, in terms of water temperature, visibility and crowding at the best rocky spots.</p>

<h2>Safety: jellyfish, currents and vigilance</h2>

<p>Snorkeling is a low-risk activity when practiced in sheltered areas, but a few precautions matter, especially for children and beginners.</p>

<ul>
  <li>Scan the water surface before getting in: visible <a href="/en/blog/meduses-dangers-marins-filfila">jellyfish</a> should prompt you to move to another spot</li>
  <li>Avoid rocky points exposed to currents or choppy water, especially outside areas used by local swimmers</li>
  <li>Never swim alone far from the shore, even at the surface</li>
  <li>Keep constant watch over children, even in calm coves</li>
  <li>Get out of the water if wind picks up suddenly, as choppy water quickly reduces visibility and comfort</li>
</ul>

<h2>Combining snorkeling with other water activities</h2>

<p>Snorkeling combines easily with other activities on the water around Filfila. A <a href="/en/blog/paddle-kayak-filfila">paddle or kayak</a> session, for example, can take you to a more distant rocky cove before stopping for a snorkeling break, a format many travelers enjoy for varying activities within a single morning.</p>

<h2>Common mistakes while snorkeling in Filfila</h2>

<p>A few mistakes keep coming up among visitors discovering snorkeling on a rocky coast for the first time. Anticipating them avoids an avoidable bad experience.</p>

<ul>
  <li>Getting into the water barefoot on the rocks without protective water shoes</li>
  <li>Choosing a poorly fitted mask that leaks and ruins the session</li>
  <li>Swimming far from shore without checking the sea conditions beforehand</li>
  <li>Expecting tropical marine life when the Mediterranean offers a different, more subtle biodiversity</li>
  <li>Forgetting waterproof sunscreen, as the back stays exposed the whole time at the surface</li>
</ul>
