<p>A traveller coming from <strong>France, Belgium or another continental European country needs no adapter at all</strong> to plug in their devices in Algeria: type C/E outlets and the 230-volt / 50 Hz power supply are identical. Only travellers coming from the United Kingdom, the United States, Canada or another country with a different plug format need to bring an adapter before departing for Skikda.</p>

<p>This question comes up often when preparing a stay at <strong>Résidence Oasis</strong>, especially among travellers who have never visited Algeria before or who are coming from outside the European zone. Poorly anticipated, it can spoil the first hours of a stay: a dead phone, a camera impossible to charge, or worse, a small appliance that isn't properly matched and burns out on first use. This article details the Algerian electrical standard, what changes depending on your country of departure, and a practical checklist for packing without any surprises.</p>

<h2>The Algerian electrical standard: type C/E outlets, 230 volts</h2>

<p>Algeria follows the most common electrical standard in continental Europe: <strong>type C and E</strong> outlets, with two round pins, delivering power at <strong>230 volts</strong> at a frequency of <strong>50 Hz</strong>. This is exactly the same format as France, Belgium, Spain or Switzerland. This shared standard greatly simplifies packing for the majority of travellers heading to Filfila, in the Skikda region.</p>

<h3>Why this standard is shared with Europe</h3>

<p>The plug format and voltage used in Algeria stem from a long historical and commercial proximity with continental Europe, which has harmonised imported electrical and household appliances over the decades. The practical result for travellers: a phone charger, a laptop or an electric shaver bought in France plugs in directly, with no transformer or shape adapter required.</p>

<h2>Travellers from continental Europe: no adapter needed</h2>

<p>For a traveller coming from <strong>France, Belgium, Spain, Italy or Germany</strong>, no adapter is necessary. The outlets in the apartments at Résidence Oasis, like the vast majority of accommodation in Algeria, accept standard European plugs directly. This also applies to most diaspora members returning home from Europe: their everyday chargers and devices work with no adaptation at all.</p>

<h2>Travellers from the UK, the US or Canada: an adapter is essential</h2>

<p>The plug format changes outside the European zone, which makes an adapter essential before departure.</p>

<h3>United Kingdom and Ireland (type G)</h3>

<p>British and Irish outlets use a three-rectangular-pin format completely different from the Algerian standard. A type G to C/E adapter is essential; since the voltage (230V/50Hz) is identical, no voltage converter is needed, only a shape adapter.</p>

<h3>United States and Canada (type A/B)</h3>

<p>North American outlets use flat two- or three-pin plugs, physically incompatible with Algerian sockets. A shape adapter is essential. Also pay attention to voltage: the North American grid runs at 110-120 volts, versus 230 volts in Algeria. Most modern chargers (phone, laptop, camera) are universal and accept both voltages, but some small appliances strictly rated for 110V require a genuine voltage converter, not just a shape adapter.</p>

<h3>How to check if a device is universal</h3>

<p>Voltage compatibility is always printed on the charger's label or casing, usually near the word "Input." A range such as "100-240V ~50/60Hz" means the device is universal and will work in Algeria with a simple shape adapter, no converter needed.</p>

<h2>A universal adapter, the most practical solution</h2>

<p>For a trip combining several legs (for instance a flight via a third country before arriving at <a href="/en/blog/quel-aeroport-pour-skikda">Constantine or Annaba airport</a>), a universal adapter remains the simplest solution: it covers most plug formats worldwide in a single compact unit, easy to slip into cabin luggage. It's also a good backup option for travellers unsure of the exact plug format they'll find on site.</p>

<h3>Power outages: a simple precaution</h3>

<p>As in many regions, a brief power outage always remains possible, particularly during periods of intense summer heat when air conditioning is heavily used. There's nothing specific to plan for beyond the usual travel precautions: keep your phone fully charged before heading out, and bring a power bank for day trips, especially toward Collo or Cap Bougaroun where charging options are more limited than at the apartment.</p>

<h2>The case of travellers from neighbouring Maghreb countries</h2>

<p>For travellers coming from Tunisia or Morocco, the question doesn't arise either: both countries share the same type C plug standard and the same 230V/50Hz voltage as Algeria. No adapter is needed for these regional trips, which simplifies preparation for a significant share of visitors to the Skikda region.</p>

<h2>A power strip, a handy accessory for a family stay</h2>

<p>Beyond the adapter itself, a small standard European power strip remains a useful accessory for a family or group stay: it allows charging several phones, a laptop and a camera at once without repeated trips to the apartment's wall outlets. It's a simple detail that makes daily life much easier during a stay in the F3 with terrace shared among several people, or for a large family staying in an F2.</p>

<h2>Electrical checklist before leaving for Skikda</h2>

<ul>
  <li>Check the plug format of your country of departure (C/E, G, A/B or other)</li>
  <li>Bring a matching shape adapter if needed, or a universal adapter</li>
  <li>Check the voltage rating printed on each device ("Input 100-240V" = universal)</li>
  <li>Bring a voltage converter only for devices strictly rated for 110-120V</li>
  <li>Pack a charged power bank for day-trip excursions</li>
  <li>Keep the adapter in cabin luggage rather than checked baggage, in case it's needed right on arrival</li>
</ul>

<h2>Common mistakes to avoid</h2>

<ul>
  <li>Confusing a shape adapter with a voltage converter: most European travellers need neither</li>
  <li>Bringing an adapter meant for another region (for example one designed for Asia) that doesn't match the Algerian format</li>
  <li>Forgetting to check the voltage rating of a North American small appliance before plugging it in directly</li>
  <li>Waiting until arrival at the apartment to discover an incompatibility, instead of checking devices before departure</li>
  <li>Neglecting the power bank for excursion days away from the apartment</li>
</ul>
