<h3>Chorba frik, the comforting evening starter</h3>

<p>Chorba frik is prepared in a simple pot from cracked wheat, meat or chicken, tomato, and fresh herbs. It is one of the most accessible recipes in an apartment, since it needs no oven or special equipment, just a medium-sized pot available in every format at the residence.</p>

<h3>Mhadjeb, the practical snack between outings</h3>

<p>Mhadjeb, a semolina crêpe stuffed with spiced tomato sauce, is cooked directly in a pan or on the hob. It's a quick option for a light lunch before heading back to the beach, or for a hearty breakfast as a change from the usual.</p>

<h3>Homemade grilled fish, the local specialty to recreate</h3>

<p>With fish bought fresh that same morning, the simplest recipe is grilled fish in the oven or on the hob, seasoned with lemon, garlic, and olive oil. For tips on where to find the freshest fish near the residence, the article on <a href="/en/blog/poisson-frais-stora">fresh fish in Stora</a> details the most reliable options.</p>

<h2>The more elaborate dishes: couscous and chakhchoukha</h2>

<p>Couscous and chakhchoukha take more time, more counter space, and ideally, for couscous, a two-tier couscoussier or, failing that, a large pot with a suitable colander for steaming the semolina.</p>

<h3>Friday couscous, holiday edition</h3>

<p>Couscous remains the ultimate sharing dish for a meal with family or friends. Preparing it requires room to spread the semolina and several containers for vegetables and meat, which is more comfortable in a spacious apartment than in a compact studio.</p>

<h3>Chakhchoukha, the crumbled flatbread in sauce</h3>

<p>Chakhchoukha, a crumbled semolina flatbread topped with a tomato and meat sauce, also needs a large serving dish and a two-stage cooking process (flatbread then sauce). Like couscous, it is better suited to group preparation, where several people can split the tasks.</p>
