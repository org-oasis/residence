<p>In Skikda, it's best to arrive with <strong>cash in Algerian dinars (DZD)</strong>: international Visa and Mastercard bank cards are accepted neither in the vast majority of shops nor at cash machines across the region. Only local Algerian cards (CIB, Edahabia) work on the national banking network. Preparing cash before departure avoids any unpleasant surprise once on site.</p>

<p>For a traveler coming from France, Europe or the Algerian diaspora, this reality often comes as a surprise: the habit of contactless payment or on-demand withdrawal doesn't apply the same way in Algeria. This article covers how the local banking system actually works, where and how to withdraw or exchange money around Skikda and Filfila, and how to concretely pay for your stay at Residence Oasis without any surprise on arrival.</p>

<h2>Why international bank cards are of limited use in Algeria</h2>

<p>The <strong>Algerian dinar (DZD)</strong> is a currency with restricted convertibility: it does not trade freely on international foreign exchange markets, and taking it out of the country is regulated. This has direct consequences for a foreign visitor. International payment networks (Visa, Mastercard) are poorly interconnected with Algerian banks for incoming transactions, which explains why a card issued in France, Belgium, Canada or elsewhere generally works neither for direct payment nor for withdrawal at a cash machine in the country.</p>

<h3>What does work: the local CIB and Edahabia cards</h3>

<p>Card payment does exist in Algeria, but through the domestic CIB (Carte Interbancaire) and Edahabia networks, tied to local bank and postal accounts. These cards are reserved for residents with an account in Algeria; they are not available to a simple visitor passing through. For a tourist or a diaspora member without a local account, they are therefore not a practical option for a one-off stay.</p>

<h3>The special case of withdrawals with a foreign card</h3>

<p>Some banks occasionally offer withdrawals with a foreign card at a handful of branches in major cities, but the service remains limited, inconsistent and unreliable for building a travel budget. The safest approach is to assume that cash machines in the Skikda region are not a fallback option for a foreign visitor, and to prepare cash in advance rather than rely on a local withdrawal.</p>

<h2>Where and how to get Algerian dinars</h2>

<p>Two options exist to obtain dinars: arrive with foreign currency (euros in particular) and exchange it on site, or bring a stock of dinars directly if your departure country's regulations allow it. Each solution has its advantages depending on the traveler's profile.</p>

<h3>The licensed exchange office, the safest option</h3>

<p>Exchanging euros for dinars at a licensed exchange office or a bank remains the most reliable route, with an official rate and a traceable transaction. Downtown Skikda, about 20 km from Filfila, holds most of the region's exchange points. It's advisable to plan this step early in the stay rather than wait until the last day, since some establishments close at weekends or on public holidays.</p>

<h3>The parallel market: a common practice worth knowing about</h3>

<p>The parallel exchange rate, more favorable than the official rate, remains a widespread economic reality in Algeria. An informed traveler will likely hear about it on site. This practice is far from anecdotal in Algeria's day-to-day economy, but it falls outside the scope of a tourist booking guide: the simplest and safest solution for a first stay remains a licensed exchange office or bringing dinars directly.</p>
