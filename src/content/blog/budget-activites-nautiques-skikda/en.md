<p>The <strong>budget for water activities in Skikda</strong> varies widely by type of outing: swimming along the 7 km of Larbi Ben M'hidi beach stays free, while renting a kayak or paddleboard, a boat excursion from Stora or a guided diving session are negotiated directly on site, with no single official price list. This guide explains how these rates work to help budget a stay in Filfila without unpleasant surprises.</p>

<p>For a traveller planning a holiday in Skikda, the question of an activities budget comes up almost as often as accommodation cost. Unlike a hotel or apartment night, usually priced in advance, the wilaya's water activities are largely offered by independent operators (fishermen, equipment rental operators, diving clubs), with rates discussed on site rather than booked online. This article details how to approach this expense, activity by activity, and how to fit it into the rest of the trip budget.</p>

<h2>Why water activities have no fixed rate in Skikda</h2>

<p>Unlike accommodation, whose nightly rate is known in advance, most water activities in Skikda wilaya work on an informal model: paddleboard and kayak rental operators set up in season on the beach, fishermen offering boat excursions from Stora harbour, or diving clubs organising trips on request. This setup, common along the whole Algerian coast, explains the absence of a single price list available before the stay.</p>

<h3>An offer that varies by season</h3>

<p>The availability and pricing of water activities logically follow the swimming season, which runs from June to November with water reaching about 26°C in August. In July and August, high demand tends to keep operators' rates at their peak, while in June or September, more available supply often allows better negotiation.</p>

<h2>Kayak and paddleboard in Filfila: how rental works</h2>

<p>Kayak and paddleboard are among the most accessible water activities in Filfila. Rental is generally by the hour, directly from operators present on the beach in high season, usually with no need to book in advance. The rate depends on the chosen duration, type of craft and time of year, with no amount posted uniformly from one operator to another.</p>

<h3>Tips for keeping this budget item under control</h3>

<ul>
  <li>Compare several operators present on the beach before renting, as rates can vary from one stand to another</li>
  <li>Favour off-peak time slots (early morning) for better equipment availability</li>
  <li>Plan the rental in June or September if date flexibility allows, as demand is lower than in full summer</li>
  <li>Negotiate a reduced rate for several consecutive hours or days</li>
</ul>

<h2>Boat excursion from Stora: a budget to negotiate on site</h2>

<p>Stora harbour remains the preferred starting point for boat excursions to the coves and sea caves of the wilaya's rocky coast. These trips are negotiated directly with fishermen or local operators present at the harbour, with no fixed price posted in advance. The price generally depends on the number of participants, which spreads the total cost of the trip, as well as the duration and distance covered along the coastline.</p>

<h3>Preparing a boat trip well</h3>

<p>Asking for several quotes on site before committing, checking the day's sea conditions and confirming the exact duration of the trip are the three habits that avoid budget surprises. A group outing, organised among several families staying together, often helps spread the total cost and reduce the individual share of the budget.</p>

<h2>Scuba diving and snorkelling: two very different budgets</h2>

<p>Snorkelling with your own mask and snorkel costs nothing once the equipment is owned or rented briefly, and already allows exploring the rocky seabed off Filfila. Scuba diving guided by a club represents a significantly higher budget, since it mobilises specialised equipment (tanks, regulators, wetsuits), qualified supervision and sometimes a boat trip to a suitable site.</p>

<p>The exact rate for a diving session depends on the club or instructor chosen and is not standardised across the wilaya. Comparing several operators, checking supervisors' qualifications and asking what is actually included (full equipment, transport, number of dives) remains essential before booking.</p>

<h2>What stays free or almost free along the coast</h2>

<p>A large part of the water enjoyment in Filfila costs nothing. Swimming at Larbi Ben M'hidi beach, about 7 km long, remains entirely free, as does walking along the coastline or watching the sunset from the sand. For a stay that needs to stay economical, alternating classic beach days with one or two occasional paid outings (boat or kayak) allows fully enjoying the sea without blowing the holiday budget.</p>

<h2>How to pay for water activities on site</h2>

<p>Independent beach operators, whether for equipment rental or excursions organised by fishermen, work almost exclusively in cash, in Algerian dinars. Card payment terminals remain rare for this kind of informal service. Bringing enough cash before heading to the beach avoids any complication when settling a rental or a trip negotiated on site.</p>

<h2>Common budget mistakes to avoid</h2>

<ul>
  <li>Assuming a rate posted online elsewhere will necessarily apply on site</li>
  <li>Not keeping any cash reserve when most activities are paid in cash</li>
  <li>Booking a boat trip or a diving session without comparing at least two operators</li>
  <li>Forgetting that July and August see the highest demand, and therefore generally the highest rates</li>
  <li>Overlooking free activities (swimming, coastal walks, personal snorkelling) which often fill much of a stay on their own</li>
</ul>
