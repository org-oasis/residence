<p>The best <strong>sunrise in Skikda</strong> is seen from the hills bordering Filfila, with an open view over the bay, or directly from Larbi Ben M'hidi beach, nearly 7 km long. Unlike the sunset, the solar disk does not rise directly over the sea: it appears behind the coastal relief to the east, while the light gradually spreads down toward the water.</p>

<p>For a traveller staying in Filfila, getting up early is not a constraint reserved for photographers. It is often the calmest moment of the day: an empty beach, still-cool air, and particularly soft light over the bay. This article covers the best viewpoints around Skikda, how sunrise differs from sunset, the most favourable season, and how to enjoy the show from your accommodation without going out.</p>

<h2>Why sunrise does not look like sunset in Filfila</h2>

<p>The layout of the Filfila coastline, facing north toward the Mediterranean, explains a difference many visitors miss. The <strong>sunset</strong> is admired facing the sea, the disk dropping toward the marine horizon. The <strong>sunrise</strong> follows the opposite logic: the sun climbs in the east, behind the hills and forest massif framing the bay, before its light spreads over the water.</p>

<h3>A gradual effect rather than a direct rise over the sea horizon</h3>

<p>This geographic shift produces a particular effect: the sky starts brightening behind the relief, clouds take on pink and orange tones before the sun is even visible, then the light gradually reaches the beach and the sea surface. Many visitors find this rendering more gradual and longer to watch than a classic sunset.</p>

<h3>What this changes for choosing a viewpoint</h3>

<p>Since the sun does not emerge directly from the sea, the best viewpoints are not necessarily right on the water. A slightly elevated spot, facing inland or east, often captures a richer palette of colours than the beach itself, where the view remains dominated by the sea to the north.</p>

<h2>The best sunrise viewpoints around Skikda</h2>

<p>Several sites stand out depending on whether you want easy access or a wider panorama over the bay.</p>

<h3>Larbi Ben M'hidi beach at daybreak</h3>

<p><a href="/en/blog/plage-larbi-ben-mhidi">Larbi Ben M'hidi beach</a> (formerly Jeanne d'Arc), nearly 7 km long, remains the most accessible option for most visitors staying in Filfila. Early in the morning, the sand is nearly empty, the air is still cool, and the low-angle light illuminates the beach without the glare that sets in by midday. It is also the calmest moment for a walk or a first swim.</p>

<h3>The hills and heights of Filfila</h3>

<p>The hills bordering the Jeanne d'Arc district offer an overview of the bay and the sea below. From these elevated spots, the spectacle of the sky changing colour behind the relief, then the light reaching the water, is generally more complete than from the beach itself. A short walk or a short drive is enough to reach these viewpoints before dawn.</p>

<h3>Stora, for a different early-morning atmosphere</h3>

<p>The <a href="/en/blog/plage-stora">Stora harbour</a> has a particular atmosphere very early in the day, with the first fishing boats getting ready to head out to sea. It is not the most spectacular site for the light itself, but the awakening harbour atmosphere adds extra interest to an early outing, especially for lovers of local-life photography.</p>

<h2>When to get up to not miss the show</h2>

<p>Sunrise time varies strongly by season at this latitude. In high summer, the sun appears as early as 5:30-6am, while in autumn and winter, the time shifts to around 7-7:30am. Either way, it remains best to arrive on site 20 to 30 minutes before the exact time, while the sky starts to change colour.</p>

<h3>Enjoying an early-morning swim</h3>

<p>During the bathing season, running from June to November, getting up early allows you to combine the sky's spectacle with a first swim before the beach fills up. In August, the water stays around 26 °C, making this early swim particularly pleasant without waiting for the sun to warm up the day.</p>

<h2>Watching the sunrise from your apartment</h2>

<p>For those who prefer not to go out so early, some accommodations allow you to enjoy the show directly from a balcony, terrace, or well-oriented window.</p>

<h3>Upper floors and open views</h3>

<p>Apartments on upper floors generally enjoy a more open view over the rooftops and surrounding greenery than ground-floor units. This doesn't guarantee a direct sea view, but remains enough to enjoy the early-morning light, coffee in hand, before starting the day.</p>

<h2>Sunrise, a favourite moment for photography</h2>

<p>Amateur and professional photographers often seek out sunrise for its soft "golden hour" light and the absence of crowds on the beach at that time. In Filfila, the combination of a relief framing the bay and an open beach offers varied framing: backlight on wet sand, silhouettes of the hills, or reflections on a still-calm sea before the wind picks up.</p>

<h3>Practical tips for an early photo outing</h3>

<ul>
  <li>Arrive at least 20 minutes before sunrise to catch the first tones in the sky</li>
  <li>Prefer a slightly elevated spot if the goal is to frame the whole bay</li>
  <li>Bring a light jacket, even in summer, as the air stays cool before the sun is high</li>
  <li>Check the weather the day before: an overcast sky greatly reduces the interest of the sunrise</li>
  <li>Avoid walking barefoot on rocky areas when light is still insufficient</li>
</ul>

<h2>Common mistakes to avoid</h2>

<p>A few mistakes come up regularly among visitors trying their first sunrise in Filfila.</p>

<ul>
  <li>Facing the sea expecting the sun to rise directly out of it, when it actually appears behind the hills to the east</li>
  <li>Leaving too late and arriving after the light has already started changing, which begins well before the solar disk appears</li>
  <li>Underestimating the morning chill, even in high summer, and going out without a light layer to cover the arms</li>
  <li>Choosing a viewpoint overshadowed by a building or relief without checking the viewing angle beforehand</li>
  <li>Not bringing a light for the way there if the chosen viewpoint requires a walk before dawn</li>
</ul>
