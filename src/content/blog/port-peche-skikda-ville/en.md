<p>Skikda's <strong>fishing harbor</strong>, particularly Stora's, remains the most reliable address in the province for buying fish fresh off the boats. Boats usually come back in late morning and early afternoon, bringing in sardine, sea bass, bream, or octopus depending on the day's catch, often at better prices than a restaurant.</p>

<p>For a traveler staying in Filfila with a fully equipped kitchen, buying fish straight from the harbor instead of ordering it already prepared changes the stay: maximum freshness, wider choice, and a budget that's easier to control. This article covers where this harbor sits, how to shop there, which species to expect by season, and how to organize an efficient outing from Résidence Oasis.</p>

<h2>Stora's fishing harbor, the go-to address for fresh fish</h2>

<p><strong>Stora's harbor</strong>, west of Skikda city, remains an active fishing harbor, not just a tourist backdrop. The colorful boats moored along the quays come back loaded with their catch, which naturally animates the seafront and directly supplies buyers and the surrounding restaurants.</p>

<h3>Still a craft-scale activity</h3>

<p>Fishing at Stora remains largely small-scale, carried out by modest boats rather than an industrial fleet. That scale has a direct benefit for the buyer: contact with the fisherman is often possible right on the quay, with no middleman, making it easy to ask about the day's catch and where it came from.</p>

<h3>When the boats come back</h3>

<p>Fishing trips most often return in late morning and early afternoon rather than at dawn. Arriving mid-morning or around midday usually offers the best selection, before the earliest buyers have already picked out the finest pieces. In high season, it's best not to wait too long into the afternoon, as tourist demand adds to the usual local clientele.</p>

<h3>Asking for the catch of the day</h3>

<p>The most reliable approach on site is to ask the fishermen or sellers directly what came in that morning, rather than aiming for one specific species in advance. That flexibility is also part of the fun of cooking on vacation: building a meal around whatever the sea offered that day.</p>

<h2>Harbor vs restaurant: comparing the budget</h2>

<p>Buying straight off the boat almost always costs less than ordering the same species already cooked at a restaurant, since preparation, service, and setting get added to the bill. In exchange, cooking it yourself takes time and a properly equipped kitchen.</p>
