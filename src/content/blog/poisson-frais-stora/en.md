<p><strong>Fresh fish in Stora</strong> is best eaten right on the quayside of the fishing port, about 20 minutes' drive from Filfila. The restaurants there sit just metres from the boats, which guarantees fish landed that same day. It is now one of the most reputed addresses in Skikda province for a seafood lunch or dinner.</p>

<p>Stora isn't a beach like the others: it's first and foremost an active fishing port, with its colourful boats, fishermen and daily market. That activity feeds directly into the restaurants along the waterfront, which source their supplies a few dozen metres from their kitchens rather than through a long logistics chain. For a visitor staying in Filfila, this article covers why Stora stands out as the go-to spot for fish, what's actually on the plate, how to pick a good address, and how to organise the trip from the residence.</p>

<h2>Why Stora is the reference for fresh fish in Skikda</h2>

<p><strong>Stora</strong>'s reputation for fish is no accident: it comes directly from having a fishing port that is still fully active, unlike other coastal areas of the province that lean more toward swimming alone.</p>

<h3>A working fishing port, not a backdrop</h3>

<p>Stora's boats come in late morning and early afternoon with the day's catch. That landing rhythm directly sets the pace for the restaurants nearby, which often build their menu around what has just arrived rather than a fixed list set in advance. That's a notable difference from a downtown restaurant, which depends on a longer delivery chain.</p>

<h3>A cluster of tables along the quay</h3>

<p>Fish restaurants are grouped along the quay, within walking distance of the moored boats. That physical closeness between catch and kitchen is the main selling point regulars point to: the trip from net to plate is measured in minutes rather than days.</p>

<h3>A menu that follows the day's catch</h3>

<p>Unlike a typical city restaurant, what's on offer in Stora depends directly on what the boats brought in. It's not unusual for a species available one day to be missing the next, which is why regulars ask directly for the day's catch rather than relying only on the printed menu.</p>

<h2>How to pick a good restaurant at Stora's port</h2>

<p>A few simple markers help tell a serious address apart from a table that relies on its quayside location alone without any real guarantee of freshness.</p>

<ul>
  <li>Fish displayed on ice, visible from outside, rather than kept hidden in the kitchen</li>
  <li>High customer turnover at lunchtime, a sign of quick stock rotation</li>
  <li>Staff able to name the species, its origin and the preparation method</li>
  <li>Prices shown by weight or clearly stated before ordering, to avoid surprises on the bill</li>
  <li>Prefer tables filled with local regulars over terraces geared mainly toward passing visitors</li>
</ul>

<h2>The port lunch ritual</h2>

<p>In Stora, lunch follows a different logic from a standard restaurant: you first choose the fish, often pointing to it directly on the ice display, then specify the cooking method (grilled, fried, in a light sauce). This market-like process is reassuring on freshness but takes a little longer than ordering off a menu at a regular restaurant. Plan on at least an hour and a half to enjoy the service properly, especially in peak season.</p>

<h3>A few mistakes to avoid</h3>

<ul>
  <li>Arriving after 2pm expecting the same choice as at midday: the best pieces go first</li>
  <li>Ordering without asking for the day's catch, relying only on a printed menu</li>
  <li>Skipping a reservation in July-August for the busiest tables on the quay</li>
  <li>Mixing up the small port stalls with the more established restaurants, whose prices and presentation differ noticeably</li>
</ul>

<h2>Getting there for visitors coming from further away</h2>

<p>For travellers arriving by plane, no airport serves Skikda directly: the two closest options are Constantine airport (Mohamed Boudiaf), about 100 km away, and Annaba airport (Rabah Bitat), about 110 km away. From either one, you then need to reach the province by road before enjoying a trip to Stora. For a stay combining beach time and food, it's worth building that road leg into the arrival plan rather than discovering it on the spot.</p>
