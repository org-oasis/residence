<p>In Skikda, the dominant <strong>traditional music</strong> combines chaâbi and so-called Constantinois music, close to the malouf of eastern Algeria, played on the oud, viola, derbouka and tar. This repertoire lives mainly at weddings and family celebrations rather than in concert halls, just 20 km from Filfila, making it an authentic musical culture that requires knowing where and how to approach it while travelling.</p>

<p>For a French-speaking traveller, a member of the Algerian diaspora, or someone curious about local culture, the traditional music of the Skikda region is often perceived from a distance, through an open window or a wedding procession, rather than experienced directly. This article details the dominant musical genres in the region, the occasions where they are really played, the characteristic instruments, and good practices for approaching them respectfully during a stay.</p>

<h2>The region's two main repertoires</h2>

<p>The Skikda region sits at the crossroads of several musical influences from eastern Algeria, which explains the coexistence of two main repertoires at local celebrations.</p>

<h3>Chaâbi, a popular urban genre</h3>

<p><strong>Chaâbi</strong> was born in Algerian popular neighbourhoods, carried by singing in Algerian Arabic and a characteristic instrument, the mandole, a kind of lute with metal strings. Its repertoire covers both religious themes and everyday subjects, and it remains very present at family celebrations along the entire eastern coast of the country, Skikda included.</p>

<h3>Constantinois music, heir to malouf</h3>

<p><strong>Constantinois music</strong>, sometimes linked to the malouf of eastern Algeria, follows a tradition closer to the Andalusian music passed down for centuries in inland cities such as Constantine. In the Skikda region, it often mixes with chaâbi at receptions, depending on family preferences and invited ensembles.</p>

<h2>Where and when you can really hear this music</h2>

<p>Unlike a concert hall where the audience watches passively, <strong>traditional music in Skikda</strong> lives above all in celebration contexts, which completely changes how to approach it while visiting.</p>

<h3>Weddings, the main source of transmission</h3>

<p>A traditional Algerian wedding in the region unfolds over several days, with a henna ceremony, an exchange of gifts between families, then a reception bringing together a chaâbi or Constantinois music ensemble depending on the family. These remain private events a passing visitor rarely attends without an invitation, though the atmosphere is sometimes perceptible from the street, through the music, the festive lights and the evening car processions.</p>

<h3>Neighbourhood celebrations and occasional events</h3>

<p>Beyond the calendar of major national holidays, some localities in the region occasionally organize neighbourhood events or moussems tied to a local tradition, sometimes linked to coastal fishing villages like Stora. The exact timing of these events varies greatly from year to year with no guaranteed fixed calendar, making an on-the-spot check essential for anyone hoping to enjoy one during their stay.</p>

<h3>Summer festivals</h3>

<p>The summer period is the most conducive to organized cultural events, open-air concerts or themed evenings, though no precise calendar can be announced months in advance. Asking on arrival remains the best way not to miss an event that coincides with your dates.</p>

<h2>Approaching this music respectfully as a visitor</h2>

<ul>
  <li>Never walk into a private wedding reception without an invitation, even if the music is audible from the street</li>
  <li>Ask a local or your accommodation's team for explanations about the meaning of the songs to enrich the listening experience</li>
  <li>Check locally for a public event before travelling specifically for a musical performance</li>
  <li>Remain discreet and respectful in case of an exceptional invitation to a family celebration</li>
  <li>Favour recorded listening (local radio, playlists) alongside an in-person experience, which is harder to guarantee in advance</li>
</ul>
