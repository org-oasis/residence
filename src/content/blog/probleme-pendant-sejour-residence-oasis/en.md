<p>If something goes wrong during the stay (air conditioning, Wi-Fi, an appliance), Résidence Oasis is reached directly by WhatsApp, Telegram or phone: the front desk for the <strong>7 apartments</strong> in Filfila stays available <strong>24/7</strong>. Booking directly, with no intermediary, makes resolving most everyday minor issues straightforward.</p>

<p>A stay in an equipped apartment, even a well-maintained one, can occasionally run into a small technical issue: air conditioning losing power, an unstable Wi-Fi connection, a question about how an appliance works. What matters most is how quick and simple the channel is to report it. This article details the contact channels available at Résidence Oasis, the most frequent issues and how to report them efficiently, and the difference between a routine technical issue and a real emergency.</p>

<h2>Contact channels in case of a problem</h2>

<p>Résidence Oasis operates on direct booking, with no gateway to a third-party platform for after-booking service. Three channels remain available to report an issue at any point during the stay: <a href="/en/contact">WhatsApp, Telegram and phone</a>.</p>

<h3>A front desk available 24/7</h3>

<p>The residence's front desk is advertised as available 24/7, covering both an issue noticed during the day and something unexpected arising late at night. WhatsApp remains the fastest channel for a first contact, particularly for sending a photo of the issue when that helps explain it.</p>

<h3>Why booking directly makes resolution easier</h3>

<p>With no platform intermediary, the guest's message reaches the team running the 7 apartments directly, with no extra approval step and no risk of information getting lost in a third-party messaging system. This is one of the concrete advantages of booking directly, alongside having no commission added on the booking's payment terms.</p>

<h2>Frequent issues and how to report them efficiently</h2>

<p>Most issues encountered in an equipped furnished rental remain minor and are resolved quickly once clearly reported. Here are the most common cases and the most effective way to describe them.</p>

<h3>Air conditioning breakdown or weak performance</h3>

<p>Specifying the room concerned (bedroom, living room) and the symptom observed (no longer cooling, unusual noise, remote not responding) allows for a faster diagnosis. In high season, air conditioning that is underperforming deserves to be reported without delay, rather than at the end of the stay.</p>

<h3>Unstable or lost Wi-Fi</h3>

<p>A weaker signal in certain rooms or an occasional dropout can happen, as with any residential connection. Noting when the dropout was observed and whether it is total or partial helps distinguish a one-off issue from a more lasting one that needs fixing.</p>

<h3>A specific appliance: jacuzzi, sauna, terrace</h3>

<p>For premium formats like the <a href="/en/apartments/f2-jacuzzi-3">F2 with private jacuzzi</a>, a question about heating, jets or filtration is reported the same way, naming the equipment involved. For the F3 with terrace, a fault on the outdoor furniture or the electric shutters is also reported through the same direct channel.</p>

<h3>Washing machine and other everyday appliances</h3>

<p>A washing machine that won't start or a faulty kitchen appliance (cooktop, oven) is reported the same way. Since these appliances are used daily, reporting them quickly limits the inconvenience for the rest of the stay.</p>

<h2>What counts as routine upkeep, and what remains the guest's responsibility</h2>

<p>A breakdown or normal wear on an appliance falls under the apartment's routine upkeep and never results in extra billing. Résidence Oasis asks for no deposit at booking, which relies on mutual trust: normal use of the apartment is never charged, while deliberate damage found afterward could be charged at checkout. This simple distinction avoids any ambiguity between an ordinary technical issue and damage caused by negligence.</p>

  <li>Keep the three contact channels (WhatsApp, Telegram, phone) saved from arrival, also useful in case of a <a href="/en/blog/arrivee-tardive-residence-oasis">late arrival at the residence</a></li>

<h2>Issues more frequent depending on the season</h2>

<p>The nature of reported issues logically varies with the time of the stay. In high season, from July to August, air conditioning runs continuously for several hours a day in most apartments, making it the appliance most often involved in a question or report. Outside the season, from October to May, exchanges focus more on heating or on the Wi-Fi connection, used more intensively during remote-work or long stays.</p>

<h3>Group stays and the F3 with terrace</h3>

<p>For a group staying in the <a href="/en/apartments/f3-terrasse-7">F3 with private terrace</a>, it helps to designate a single point of contact to centralise exchanges with the residence in case of a problem, rather than several group members separately contacting the same channels. This avoids redundant messages and speeds up handling, particularly when several extra guests are declared on the apartment.</p>

<h2>What to do if a problem takes time to resolve</h2>

<p>Most minor issues are settled in a few message exchanges. If a problem persists despite an initial report, it helps to follow up on the same contact channel with a clear reminder of the date and time of the first message, rather than switching channels or waiting until the end of the stay to raise it again. Documenting the issue with a photo or a short, dated description also helps with any later exchange, including questions about the <a href="/en/blog/politique-annulation-residence-oasis">stay's general terms</a>.</p>

A missing item or a visible defect reported at that exact moment is almost always resolved faster than a late report at the end of the stay, and avoids any ambiguity about the apartment's condition on arrival as well as on departure.</p>
