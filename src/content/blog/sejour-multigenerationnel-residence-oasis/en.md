<p>A <strong>multi-generational stay at Résidence Oasis</strong> is usually built around the F3 with private terrace, which sleeps 8 as base capacity and up to 10 with the 2 extra guests allowed. Two separate bedrooms keep generations apart at night, while the terrace acts as a shared dining room for family meals — a format designed to bring grandparents, parents and children together under one roof.</p>

<p>Bringing three generations together for the holidays comes with specific constraints: grandparents look for calm and few stairs, parents want a space to unwind, and children need room to play without disturbing the rest of the group. This article explains how to size the accommodation, split the budget and organise the day-to-day for a stay that works for everyone, from the youngest to the oldest.</p>

<h2>Why the F3 with terrace remains the reference format</h2>

<p>Among the residence's seven units, the <a href="/en/apartments/f3-terrasse-7">F3 with private terrace</a> is the only one sized to host an extended family under one roof without sacrificing anyone's privacy. The two separate bedrooms allow, for instance, grandparents on one side and parents with the youngest children on the other, while the living room and terrace remain shared spaces for the whole group.</p>

<h3>Two bedrooms, one shared living space</h3>

<p>Splitting the unit into two separate bedrooms avoids the classic pitfall of a single living room where everyone sleeps on extra mattresses. Each generation gets a private space to rest, while the private terrace naturally becomes the place for meals and evening conversation, much like a family house's courtyard.</p>

<h3>A capacity built for 8, extendable to 10</h3>

<p>The base capacity of 8 already covers most extended-family setups: two grandparents, two parents and four children, for instance. For larger families, up to 2 extra guests can be added for a surcharge, bringing the maximum capacity to 10 occupants in the same unit.</p>

<h2>What to do if the group is bigger than the F3's capacity</h2>

<p>Beyond 10 people, a single unit is no longer comfortable enough. The most practical solution is to book a second apartment in the residence, an F2 for example, to house part of the group while keeping the F3 and its terrace as the gathering point for meals and shared moments. This approach, covered in <a href="/en/blog/louer-plusieurs-appartements-groupe-skikda">this article on renting several apartments as a group</a>, lets each sub-group keep its privacy at night without scattering the family across several different addresses in town.</p>

<h2>Grandparents with reduced mobility</h2>

<p>The F3 with terrace sits on the top floor of the residence, which can be a constraint for a grandparent who would rather avoid daily stairs. In that case, booking the <a href="/en/apartments/studio-deluxe-1">furnished studio for two</a> on the ground floor alongside it lets that family member enjoy step-free access while staying in the same residence as the rest of the group, just a few metres from the F3 and its terrace.</p>

<h2>Budgeting for a multi-generational stay</h2>

<p>The F3 with terrace's base rate, outside peak season, starts from {{TIER_F3_EUR}} € / {{TIER_F3_DZD}} DA a night for a capacity of 8, a very competitive per-person cost once split across the whole group. A 10% discount applies automatically from 7 nights, a threshold often reached naturally during a one- to two-week summer family stay.</p>

<h3>Booking without surprises for the whole group</h3>

<p>Booking goes directly through <a href="/en/contact">WhatsApp, Telegram and phone</a>, with a one-night deposit paid by CCP bank transfer to secure the dates. The balance is paid on arrival, in dinars or euros, with no security deposit required. One person in the group can centralise all communication with the residence's team, which simplifies things when several households share the same stay.</p>

<h2>Choosing the right time of year for a multi-generational stay</h2>

<p>The swimming season in Skikda runs from June to November, with water reaching around 26°C in August. July and August remain the most requested months, partly because they match school holidays and the summer return of family members living abroad, but the most intense heat of that period rarely suits the older members of the group.</p>

<h3>June, September and October for more comfort</h3>

<p>For a grandparent sensitive to heat, planning the stay in June or September-October offers sea water that is still pleasant without the peak temperatures of July-August, along with lower rates and a generally quieter residence. This timing also leaves more flexibility to secure the F3 with terrace at short notice, unlike the height of summer when bookings are often made weeks in advance.</p>

<h3>July-August when the whole family must align schedules</h3>

<p>When the stay depends on several households' school and work holidays, July-August is often the only common window available. In that case, booking early becomes essential to secure the F3 with terrace rather than ending up splitting the group across two separate units due to lack of availability.</p>

<h2>Organising day-to-day life across generations</h2>

<p>Beyond the accommodation, a successful multi-generational stay also depends on the pace set for the days. Grandparents generally appreciate calm mornings and a nearby beach, while children need more active afternoon activities.</p>

<ul>
  <li>Morning at Filfila beach, 700 m from the residence, before the strongest heat</li>
  <li>Midday meal together on the F3 terrace, practical for gathering the whole group around one table</li>
  <li>A quieter afternoon for the elders, while part of the group heads out on an excursion to Stora or Collo</li>
  <li>Evenings on the terrace once the heat drops, usually the moment most appreciated across all generations</li>
</ul>

<h2>Common mistakes to avoid</h2>

<ul>
  <li>Booking only an F2 for a group of 8 to save on budget, at the cost of real discomfort at night</li>
  <li>Not telling the team the exact number of extra guests before arrival</li>
  <li>Forgetting the floor constraint for a grandparent and discovering it only on arrival</li>
  <li>Underestimating the time needed to coordinate meal schedules across generations with different rhythms</li>
  <li>Waiting until the last minute to book during peak summer, when demand for the F3 is highest</li>
</ul>
