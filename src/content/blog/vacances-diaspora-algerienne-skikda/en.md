<p>A <strong>homeland holiday in Skikda</strong> is planned like any organised trip: choosing an arrival airport between Constantine (about 100 km away) and Annaba (about 110 km away), since no airport serves Skikda itself, then deciding between staying with family and booking an independent apartment. More and more diaspora families combine both, with accommodation in Filfila, 700 m from the sea, for the days they want their own pace.</p>

<p>Coming back every summer to see parents, grandparents and cousins is always a moment to look forward to, but the practical organisation often raises the same questions: where to sleep without overcrowding a family home that is already full, how to budget for a stay that can last several weeks, and which apartment size suits a group spanning several generations. This article covers the practical markers for organising a homeland trip to Skikda without surprises, from the airport arrival to booking the apartment.</p>

<h2>Why more families combine the family home with an independent apartment</h2>

<p>The classic "everything at the family home" arrangement is still common, but it shows its limits when several households return the same week. A house that usually holds 4 or 5 people suddenly has 15 to 20 relatives gathered for the occasion, which complicates sleeping arrangements, the bathroom and daily meals.</p>

<h3>Modern comfort alongside family hospitality</h3>

<p>An independent apartment with air conditioning, Wi-Fi and a fitted kitchen makes it easier to find a more restful holiday rhythm after days spent with the family. Children sleep more easily in a quiet room, parents get a private bathroom, and nobody feels obliged to camp on the living-room sofa for two weeks.</p>

<h3>Staying independent without moving away from relatives</h3>

<p>Staying in Filfila does not cut you off from family settled in town or a nearby village: the distance is generally covered by car in under an hour depending on the neighbourhood, which leaves the freedom to spend the day with relatives and come back to sleep in a quiet place, 700 m from the sea, ready for the beach the next morning.</p>

<h2>How to reach Skikda from abroad: the two reference airports</h2>

<p>The logistical starting point of a homeland trip is choosing the arrival airport, since no airport directly serves Skikda. Two options exist, both roughly an hour and a half's drive from Filfila depending on traffic.</p>

<h3>Constantine Mohamed Boudiaf Airport</h3>

<p>Located about 100 km from Filfila, Constantine airport is usually the better served from France and other European countries, with more frequencies during the summer. It is generally the first option to check when comparing flight prices for a large family.</p>

<h3>Annaba Rabah Bitat Airport</h3>

<p>Annaba airport, about 110 km from Filfila, is a useful alternative when flights to Constantine are full or pricier on the desired dates, which happens often during the July-August peak linked to the mass return of the diaspora.</p>

<h2>What to do during a homeland holiday in Skikda</h2>

<p>Beyond family reunions, a stay in Skikda usually leaves time to rediscover the region, especially for children born or raised abroad who know little of their parents' coastline. The swimming season runs from June to November, with water reaching around 26°C in August, leaving a wide window to combine the beach and sightseeing.</p>

<ul>
  <li>Beach days in Filfila, a few minutes from the accommodation</li>
  <li>A trip to Stora harbour and a boat excursion to the rocky coves</li>
  <li>A day in Collo or at Cap Bougaroun for families with a car</li>
  <li>An afternoon at Marina d'Or Water Park or Rusica Park for the children</li>
  <li>Extended family meals, usually easier to organise on an F3 terrace than in a cramped living room</li>
</ul>

<h2>Common mistakes when returning home to Skikda</h2>

<p>A few mistakes come up regularly among diaspora families who organise their stay in the rush of the final months before departure.</p>

<ul>
  <li>Booking the apartment too late for July-August, the period when demand linked to the diaspora's return is highest</li>
  <li>Underestimating the number of people actually present at family meals and booking a format that is too small</li>
  <li>Not confirming in advance the number of extra guests allowed and the related surcharge</li>
  <li>Forgetting that the balance of the stay is paid on arrival and not bringing enough cash or a working card</li>
  <li>Overlooking the travel time from Constantine or Annaba airport when planning the first day</li>
</ul>
