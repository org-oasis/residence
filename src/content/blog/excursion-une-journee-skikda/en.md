<p>From Filfila, the most common day trips range from Stora, about 20 minutes away, to Constantine, roughly a hundred kilometres and nearly two hours of driving. In between, Collo, Cap Bougaroun or the inland waterfalls cover very different types of outings, from wild coastline to the cool air of the hills.</p>

<p>The Skikda wilaya offers no shortage of options for stepping away from the beach-and-apartment routine for a few hours. The real challenge is choosing: some excursions fit comfortably into half a day, while others call for an early start and several hours of driving each way. This article reviews the most realistic destinations from Skikda and Filfila, the travel time to expect, what there is to see on site, and a few pointers for planning the outing without losing an entire day in the car.</p>

<h2>Criteria for choosing a day trip</h2>

<p>Not every excursion suits every trip, depending on the time available, the age of any children travelling, or the mood of the day. Three simple criteria help narrow down the options below.</p>

<h3>Acceptable travel time for the day</h3>

<p>A good excursion leaves time on site, not just in the car. For a trip under an hour, like Stora, the day stays flexible. Beyond an hour and a half to two hours, as with Collo, Constantine or Annaba, it's best to leave early in the morning and accept a late-afternoon or evening return.</p>

<h3>Sea, nature or heritage</h3>

<p>Excursions fall into three families: the coastline (Stora, Collo, Cap Bougaroun), inland nature (waterfalls, cooler hill air), and urban heritage (Constantine, Annaba). Alternating between families over a week-long stay avoids the fatigue of stringing together identical beach days.</p>

<h2>Stora, the closest excursion to Filfila</h2>

<p>Stora, west of Skikda, is the simplest outing to organise from Filfila: about 20 minutes by car, with no need for an especially early start. The active fishing harbour, colourful boats and fresh fish restaurants give Stora an atmosphere quite different from Filfila's purely seaside beaches.</p>

<h3>A boat excursion from the harbour</h3>

<p>Stora remains the most convenient starting point for a boat excursion to the coves and sea caves of the rocky coastline, an activity that breaks from the classic beach routine and is usually arranged on site with local providers at the harbour.</p>

<h3>A half-day programme</h3>

<p>A harbour morning and a boat trip, fresh fish for lunch facing the quays, and a return to Filfila in the early afternoon to still enjoy the beach: Stora fits well into a short outing that doesn't eat into the rest of the day.</p>

<h2>Collo, the wilaya's wild coastline</h2>

<p>Further west, Collo offers a less developed coastline and scenery quite different from Filfila and Stora. The road there follows part of the coast and takes over an hour, making Collo a full excursion in its own right rather than a simple mid-morning detour. The route, what to see on site and good practices for planning the day are covered in a <a href="/en/blog/visiter-collo-depuis-skikda">dedicated guide on how to visit Collo from Skikda</a>.</p>

<h2>Cap Bougaroun, the coastline's furthest point</h2>

<p>Cap Bougaroun marks one of the northernmost points on the Algerian coastline. Its rugged relief, lighthouse and views over the Mediterranean make it a popular excursion from Filfila, usually organised over a full day given the distance and winding roads leading there. The drive itself, along the cliffside, is part of the experience.</p>

<h2>Into the hinterland: waterfalls and cool air</h2>

<p>The Skikda wilaya isn't limited to its coastline. Inland, several waterfalls offer a refreshing alternative to beach days, particularly welcome in high summer when the midday heat becomes more noticeable. These sites usually require a vehicle and part of the day devoted to driving and a short approach walk, but remain reachable as a round trip from Filfila.</p>

<h2>Constantine, the city of suspended bridges</h2>

<p>Constantine, roughly a hundred kilometres from Filfila and about two hours of driving, is a completely different register: a city on the high plateaus known for its bridges suspended above the Rhumel gorges, offering urban heritage unlike anything on the coast. It's also home to the closest airport to Skikda, making it a familiar stop for travellers arriving or leaving by plane. A day trip remains possible, but leaves little time for sightseeing once the round-trip drive is deducted; staying overnight allows a more relaxed visit to the city.</p>

<h2>Annaba, between heritage and seafront</h2>

<p>Annaba, about 110 km from Filfila, also combines heritage with a coastal setting. The longer drive compared to Stora or Collo mostly appeals to travellers wanting to discover another Algerian coastal city during their stay, or to those arriving via Annaba's airport (Rabah Bitat) who want to spend a few hours there before heading on to Filfila.</p>

<h2>Planning your excursion budget</h2>

<p>The cost of a day trip mainly comes down to transport: fuel or a car rental, possibly a long-distance taxi for Collo or Cap Bougaroun. On top of that, add lunch on site and, for Stora, the boat excursion rate negotiated directly with the provider at the harbour. Building in a margin for the unexpected (a schedule change, an extra stop along the way) is good practice, especially for destinations over an hour away.</p>

<ul>
