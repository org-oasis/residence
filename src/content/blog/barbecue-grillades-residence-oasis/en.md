<p><strong>No</strong>: Résidence Oasis does not provide a charcoal or gas barbecue, as open flames are banned on the balconies and terraces of Filfila's multi-unit buildings for fire-safety reasons. The <strong>7 apartments</strong> instead have a fully equipped kitchen, and the F3's private terrace remains ideal for an outdoor meal.</p>

<p>The question comes up often among travellers picturing a seaside stay with open-air grilling, especially in summer or during a stay with friends or an extended family. In Filfila, as in most furnished residences on the Algerian coast, the reality is more regulated than the image of grilling on the beach. This article explains why a classic barbecue is not offered, what alternatives exist on site for an outdoor meal, and how to still organise a convivial evening around local cooking.</p>

<h2>Why barbecuing is regulated in Filfila's furnished residences</h2>

<p>Filfila's professional furnished residences, Résidence Oasis among them, are multi-unit buildings where several apartments share façades, adjoining balconies, or terraces close to one another. This setup changes things compared with a standalone villa with a private garden, where a traditional barbecue poses fewer constraints.</p>

<h3>Fire safety and good neighbourliness</h3>

<p>An open flame, glowing coals, or a gas canister handled at height carry a risk of fire and smoke spreading to neighbouring units. This is the main reason a charcoal or gas barbecue is not allowed on the residence's balconies and terraces, a rule common to this type of multi-unit housing rather than a quirk of one property.</p>

<h3>What changes depending on the apartment format</h3>

<p>The <a href="/en/apartments/f3-terrasse-7">F3 with private terrace</a> offers the residence's largest outdoor space, built for a group meal rather than open-flame cooking. The studio and F2 formats have smaller balconies, enough for a coffee outdoors but less suited to laying a full table for several guests.</p>

<h2>Alternatives for cooking and eating outdoors at the residence</h2>

<p>The absence of a classic barbecue does not mean there's no option for enjoying the terrace or balcony at mealtimes. Several solutions recreate the spirit of an outdoor meal without the safety constraints of an open flame.</p>

<h3>Each apartment's fully equipped kitchen</h3>

<p>Every apartment at the residence has a <a href="/en/apartments">fully equipped kitchen</a>: stovetop, oven, fridge, and basic utensils let you prepare grilled meat, fish, and vegetables indoors before setting the meal out on the terrace or balcony. The article on <a href="/en/blog/equipements-appartements-skikda">the equipment provided in the apartments</a> details the full room-by-room inventory. It's the simplest solution for a convivial dinner with no extra gear to bring.</p>

<h3>A tabletop electric griddle as a backup</h3>

<p>For those wanting something closer to the sensation of outdoor grilling, a tabletop electric griddle or a small flame-free appliance remains compatible with using a terrace in a multi-unit residence. This type of appliance heats a metal plate with no combustion or flying embers, sharply limiting the risk compared with a traditional barbecue.</p>

<h2>Organising a convivial meal without an open-flame barbecue</h2>

<p>A stay with friends or an extended family doesn't need a real barbecue to make a mealtime special. Traditional Algerian cooking actually lends itself very well to oven or pan preparation, without losing flavour compared with cooking over coals.</p>

<h3>Grilling fresh fish bought locally</h3>

<p>Fresh fish, bought in the morning at <a href="/en/blog/poisson-frais-stora">Stora</a> harbour about twenty minutes from Filfila or from the convenience shops in the Jeanne d'Arc district, is very easy to prepare in the apartment's oven or on the stovetop. Grilled sardines, sea bream, or red mullet are then enjoyed at the table on the F3's terrace, with an open view of the surroundings.</p>

<h3>Marinating meat and vegetables before cooking</h3>

<p>A simple marinade (olive oil, lemon, local spices) prepared the day before shortens same-day cooking time and concentrates the flavours, whether cooking in the oven, in a pan, or on an electric griddle. It's a practical trick for a large group that would rather spend time at the table than in the kitchen.</p>

<h2>Having a real barbecue elsewhere in Skikda: precautions to know</h2>

<p>For travellers set on an open-flame barbecue, the question then shifts to the province's outdoor spaces rather than the residence itself. Caution still applies: natural areas such as the Filfila reserve or wooded surroundings are sensitive to fire risk, particularly during the hottest and driest summer months. It's worth checking locally before lighting any outdoor fire, avoiding open flames in strong wind, and favouring designated areas where they exist rather than an improvised fire.</p>
