<p><strong>Beach volleyball and beach sports in Filfila</strong> are played freely on Larbi Ben M'hidi beach, about 7 km long, away from supervised swimming zones. No fixed court with a permanent net is guaranteed on-site: groups wanting an organized match usually bring their own portable net, rackets, or ball, with the sand offering plenty of room to improvise a court.</p>

<p>For a group or extended-family stay in Filfila, beach sports are a great complement to swimming and sunbathing. Beach volleyball, rackets, sand pétanque, or a simple ball game require no club membership or license, just a bit of space and, where needed, light gear that's easy to pack. This article covers where to play on the beach, what gear to bring, the most comfortable hours, and how to run a session without disturbing other beachgoers.</p>

<h2>Larbi Ben M'hidi beach, Filfila's best playing field</h2>

<p>At about <strong>7 km</strong> long, Larbi Ben M'hidi beach (formerly Jeanne d'Arc) remains the best-suited site for improvising a beach volleyball match or ball games in Filfila. Its length makes it easy to find a clear stretch, especially away from the busiest swimming areas.</p>

<h3>Picking a suitable stretch of beach</h3>

<p>For an improvised beach volleyball court, it's best to favor a stretch of firm sand, away from supervised swimming zones and the busiest walking paths. The ends of the beach, less crowded in peak season, generally offer more room to set up a net without disturbing swimmers or other groups already settled in.</p>

<h3>No permanent dedicated court</h3>

<p>Unlike some seaside resorts that install fixed beach volleyball courts with nets and ground markings, Filfila has no permanent public setup of that kind. That doesn't prevent play: a portable net, staked into the sand, is enough to mark out a temporary court for the length of a session.</p>

<h3>Why it's better to bring your own gear</h3>

<p>Sports shops and beachfront stores in Skikda occasionally carry basic balls or rackets, but the selection stays limited compared to major Mediterranean resorts. For a reliable session, especially in peak season when local stock sells out fast, bringing your own gear in your luggage remains the safest option.</p>

<h2>Other beach sports you can play in Filfila</h2>

<p>Beyond beach volleyball, several activities work well for free play on the sand, with no license or organized structure required.</p>

<h3>Beach rackets, simple to improvise</h3>

<p>Beach racket games (sometimes called paddle ball) only need two rackets and a light ball or shuttle. It's one of the easiest activities to fit between two swims, playable solo in pairs or in a small group taking turns.</p>

<h3>Pétanque on firm sand</h3>

<p>Pétanque is traditionally played on a compacted surface, but an adapted version on firm sand near the water's edge still works well, especially in the late afternoon when the sand is more compact. It's an activity that spans generations, suited to both children and grandparents on a multi-generational stay.</p>

<h3>Frisbee and free ball games</h3>

<p>Frisbee and free ball games remain the most accessible options, with no strict rules or specialized gear. They can be played on any clear stretch of beach, simply avoiding busy swimming zones and other visitors' towels.</p>

<h2>Best times for beach sports in summer</h2>

<p>In peak summer, choosing the right time slot largely determines how comfortable beach sports feel. Between noon and 5pm, the heat and hot sand make prolonged physical effort uncomfortable, even risky for barefoot children.</p>

<h3>Before 10am or after 5pm</h3>

<p>Morning slots before 10am and late-afternoon slots after 5pm remain the most comfortable for a beach volleyball or racket session, with cooler sand and a more bearable outside temperature for sustained effort. Outside peak season, these hours can stretch further, since the heat is generally milder.</p>

<h3>Adjusting intensity to the weather</h3>

<p>During a heat spike, it's better to shorten sessions and take more breaks in the shade or water rather than keeping a constant intensity. The article on <a href="/en/blog/activites-canicule-skikda">activities in Skikda during a heatwave</a> covers the right habits to adopt when temperatures climb sharply.</p>

<h2>Beach sports with the family: adapting play for children</h2>

<p>For children, an adapted version of beach sports works well from about age 5-6, with a lighter ball and simplified rules. Full beach volleyball, with a regulation net and scorekeeping, suits teenagers and adults looking for more structured play.</p>

<h2>Good practices and beach etiquette</h2>

<p>A beach volleyball or ball game session shares space with other beach users: swimmers, families settled nearby, and walkers. A few simple rules avoid friction and keep the general atmosphere pleasant.</p>

<ul>
  <li>Choose a clear stretch of beach, away from already-set-up towels and umbrellas</li>
  <li>Avoid playing directly in or right next to supervised swimming zones</li>
  <li>Always retrieve balls and frisbees that drift toward neighboring groups</li>
  <li>Take down the portable net and pack up the gear when leaving the beach, leaving nothing behind</li>
  <li>Adjust volume and intensity of play when families with very young children are nearby</li>
</ul>

<h2>Common mistakes to avoid</h2>

<ul>
  <li>Counting on renting gear locally without checking availability beforehand, especially in peak season</li>
  <li>Playing beach volleyball in direct midday sun without sufficient protection or hydration</li>
  <li>Setting up an improvised court too close to a busy swimming zone, risking disturbing other beachgoers</li>
  <li>Forgetting water shoes or sandals for walking on hot sand between sessions</li>
  <li>Neglecting to bring a spare ball or net for a multi-day group stay</li>
</ul>
