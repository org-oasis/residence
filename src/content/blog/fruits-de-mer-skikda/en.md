<h2>Seafood or fish: a useful distinction</h2>

<p>In the coastal cuisine of the Skikda region, the term <strong>seafood</strong> refers to a category distinct from fish proper. While fish covers species such as sea bass, sea bream or sardine, seafood brings together squid, shrimp, octopus and shellfish such as mussels.</p>

<h3>Different preparations</h3>

<p>The two categories are prepared differently: fish is most often grilled, pan-fried or baked whole in the oven, while seafood comes fried, grilled or stewed in a tomato sauce with local spices. A full meal frequently combines both, with a seafood starter followed by a fish main course.</p>

<h3>Why this distinction matters when ordering</h3>

<p>Knowing this distinction avoids disappointment when ordering: a visitor who simply asks for "fish" at a harbour restaurant may be offered a plate of squid or shrimp depending on the day's catch, since both categories often arrive on the same boats.</p>

<h2>Stora harbour, the reference address for seafood</h2>

<p><a href="/en/blog/poisson-frais-stora">Stora harbour</a> holds the region's best seafood addresses, about 20 minutes by car from the Jeanne d'Arc district in Filfila. The restaurants along the quay serve a selection that depends directly on the catch brought in that same morning.</p>

<h3>What you typically find in Stora</h3>

<p>Fried or grilled squid and garlic shrimp are among the most common preparations, joined depending on the season by stewed octopus or mussels. Freshness remains the main selling point: the products come straight from boats moored just metres from the tables.</p>

<h3>Timing and crowds</h3>

<p>Boats generally come back in late morning and early afternoon, which makes a slightly later lunch often more rewarding than an overly early meal if you want the day's catch. In the evening, the most reputed tables fill up fast during high season.</p>

<h2>Other addresses along the coast: Collo and downtown</h2>

<p>Beyond Stora, <a href="/en/blog/poisson-frais-collo">Collo</a>, further west along the coast, also offers seafood restaurants tied to its own fishing harbour, in a wilder setting less frequented by tourists. Downtown Skikda, about 20 km from Filfila, also has a few restaurants that work with seafood, useful for an outing combined with a visit to the urban heritage sites or a shopping trip.</p>

<h2>Seasonality and crowds</h2>

<p>Fishing continues year-round in the Skikda region, but supply and freshness vary with the season and sea conditions. The period from June to November, which matches the swimming season, concentrates the highest tourist numbers and the widest seafood variety in restaurants. Outside this season, the offering narrows but the atmosphere stays more authentic, with a mostly local clientele.</p>

<h2>Budget and organizing the meal</h2>

<p>The budget for a seafood meal varies widely depending on the choice between restaurant and home cooking, the type of seafood chosen, and the season. For a budget-conscious group, splitting meals between one or two restaurant outings at the harbour and home preparations with freshly bought products remains the most balanced formula over a week-long stay.</p>
