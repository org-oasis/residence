<h2>What Is an Aqiqa, and Why Host It in Filfila</h2>

<p>The aqiqa is a tradition widespread among Muslim families, marking a child's birth with a family meal and, depending on each family's customs, a ritual sacrifice around the seventh day after birth. It's a gathering rather than a formal reception, often limited to close parents, grandparents, and a few members of the extended family.</p>

<p>For a family already staying in Filfila, or choosing this very moment to bring together relatives traveling from afar, hosting the aqiqa on site avoids having to improvise a venue elsewhere. The residence has seven independent apartments on the same site, leaving several options depending on the size of the family circle gathered for the occasion, much like other <a href="/en/blog/fete-fiancailles-residence-oasis">family celebrations hosted in Filfila</a>, from the khotba to the <a href="/en/blog/fete-mariage-residence-oasis">wedding</a>.</p>

<h2>Is There a Fixed Date to Respect for the Ceremony?</h2>

<p>Tradition places the aqiqa around the seventh day after birth, but in practice, many families adjust the date based on everyone's availability, the health of the mother and child, or the ability to gather relatives coming from afar. Holding the ceremony during a stay already planned in Filfila, rather than on a fixed date, often makes it easier to bring more people together in better conditions.</p>

<p>Part of the sacrificial meat is traditionally shared with relatives, neighbors, and sometimes families in need, beyond the meal consumed on site. This sharing aspect remains handled by the family itself, independent of the apartment chosen to host the family part of the day.</p>
