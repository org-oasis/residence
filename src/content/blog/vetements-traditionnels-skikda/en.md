<h3>The karakou, centrepiece for major occasions</h3>

<p>The karakou, a short velvet jacket embroidered with gold or silver thread, remains the most iconic piece for Algerian weddings and engagements. Its making, often done by hand, can take anywhere from several days to several weeks depending on how elaborate the embroidery is, which means any custom order needs to be planned well ahead.</p>

<h3>The gandoura, for more everyday use</h3>

<p>The gandoura, a long dress with a simpler cut, is worn both daily and for less formal family gatherings. It comes in many regional variants, with fabrics and patterns that change from one wilaya to another.</p>

<h3>Haik and burnous, Algeria's textile heritage</h3>

<p>The haik, a large traditional white veil, and the burnous, a men's wool coat worn at ceremonies or in the cool season, are rarer in everyday use today but remain sought after by families attached to dress heritage, particularly as a symbolic piece passed down between generations.</p>

<h2>Where to look for these garments around Skikda</h2>

<p>The spread of the offer follows the same pattern as the <a href="/en/blog/souks-marches-skikda">traditional markets of Skikda</a>: Filfila stays limited to everyday local shops, while downtown holds most of the textile and tailoring offer.</p>

<h3>Central Skikda, the main textile hub</h3>

<p>The shopping streets around the downtown market bring together fabric shops, haberdashers, and tailoring workshops. This is where the widest choice of ready-made models is found, along with the tailors able to make a custom piece, particularly an embroidered karakou.</p>

<h3>Filfila, more limited options</h3>

<p>Filfila doesn't offer a real selection of traditional dress at neighbourhood scale. Travellers staying at the residence looking for a specific piece are therefore better off planning a trip to central Skikda, ideally combined with another heritage or market outing.</p>

<h2>Ready-made or custom-tailored: what to choose depending on your stay</h2>

<p>Choosing between an already-made piece and a custom order largely depends on how much time is available during the stay.</p>

<h3>For a short stay</h3>

<p>A gandoura or a ready-made karakou model found directly in a shop remains the most realistic option for a stay of a few days. Trying on several sizes and models on site avoids sizing surprises after returning home.</p>

<h3>For a longer stay</h3>

<p>A stay of a week or more leaves more room to consider a custom piece, especially if the first contact with the tailor or embroiderer happens in the early days of the trip. The 10% discount granted from 7 nights booked at Residence Oasis makes this kind of longer, more deliberate stay easier to plan.</p>

<h2>Budget to plan for</h2>

<p>The price of traditional dress varies widely depending on the fabric, the richness of the embroidery, and the finish. A simple ready-made gandoura remains noticeably more affordable than a hand-embroidered custom karakou, which represents a bigger investment given the artisanal work involved. Comparing several shops downtown and asking for a detailed quote before committing remains the best approach, especially for a piece meant for a wedding or an important occasion.</p>

<h2>The accessories that complete the outfit</h2>

<p>Traditional dress is rarely worn alone: several accessories go with it, usually found in the same shops or in nearby stalls in central Skikda.</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Embroidered babouches</strong>: soft slippers, often matched to the colour of the outfit</li>
  <li><strong>Traditional jewellery</strong>: silver or gold-toned pieces, sometimes Berber-inspired, worn on major occasions</li>
  <li><strong>Scarves and mdharma</strong>: embroidered pieces of fabric worn as a headpiece to complete the karakou</li>
  <li><strong>Embroidered belts</strong>: accessories that mark the waist on a gandoura or karakou</li>
</ul>

<p>Planning some time to look for these accessories alongside the main garment avoids having to improvise at the last minute, especially if the outfit is meant for a specific ceremony during the stay.</p>
