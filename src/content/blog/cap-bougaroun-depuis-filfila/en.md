<p>Cap Bougaroun is reached from Filfila via a coastal road that passes through Collo before climbing to the northernmost tip of the Algerian coast. The trip requires a full day round-trip: the stated distance matters less than the winding final kilometres, which noticeably slow the pace all the way to the lighthouse.</p>

<p>For a stay built around the beaches of Filfila, Cap Bougaroun offers one of the most spectacular outings in the Skikda wilaya: a lighthouse among the tallest in Algeria, cliffs plunging into the Mediterranean, and a panorama that breaks from the seaside rhythm of the Jeanne d'Arc neighbourhood. This article covers the route from Filfila, what there is to see on site, the precautions to take, and how to plan the day without rushing.</p>

<h2>Where Cap Bougaroun is located</h2>

<p>Cap Bougaroun marks one of the northernmost points of the Algerian coast, west of the Skikda wilaya, beyond Collo. Its rugged terrain and relative isolation make it a very different site from the developed beaches of Filfila or Stora: here, the appeal lies in the raw scenery more than in on-site amenities.</p>

<h3>The drive from Filfila</h3>

<p>From the Jeanne d'Arc neighbourhood in Filfila, the road first follows the coastline toward Collo before turning off onto a narrower, winding road leading to the tip of the cape. This final stretch, along the cliffside, calls for attentive driving and noticeably slows the pace compared with a trip on a standard road, even over a seemingly modest distance.</p>

<h3>Own car, rental or taxi?</h3>

<p>A vehicle is essential for this trip: no regular public transport line serves Cap Bougaroun from Filfila. A personal car or a <strong>car rental</strong> for the day are the two realistic options; a long-distance taxi arranged in advance is possible but costly given the length of the ride.</p>

<h2>What to see and do at Cap Bougaroun</h2>

<p>The site is visited above all for its panorama and lighthouse, in a preserved natural setting quite different from the wilaya's busiest seaside areas.</p>

<h3>The lighthouse, among the tallest in Algeria</h3>

<p>The Cap Bougaroun lighthouse stands at the tip of the cape and ranks among the tallest on the Algerian coast. Its position atop steep cliffs offers an open view over the Mediterranean that alone justifies the detour for lovers of coastal scenery.</p>

<h3>Cliffs and a Mediterranean panorama</h3>

<p>The cape's rugged terrain, with cliffs plunging directly into the sea, makes it a favourite among hikers and photographers. The absence of heavy tourist development preserves a wild character that contrasts with Filfila's residential area.</p>

<h2>When to visit Cap Bougaroun: high season or off-season</h2>

<p>As with Collo, the <strong>bathing season on the wilaya's coastline runs from June to November</strong>, but Cap Bougaroun is visited above all for its panorama rather than for swimming. Outside July-August, the winding road is quieter and driving more comfortable; in summer, it's best to leave early to avoid the midday heat on the final, shadeless kilometres.</p>

<h2>What to prepare before heading to Cap Bougaroun</h2>

<p>A trip to Cap Bougaroun takes more preparation than a short outing like Stora, given the longer drive and the limited shops available on site.</p>

<h3>Fuel, water and snacks</h3>

<p>Filling up before leaving Filfila avoids having to look for a petrol station along a road where options are limited, especially past Collo. Bringing enough water and snacks is essential, as the lighthouse site itself has no shop.</p>

<h3>Suitable footwear and sun protection</h3>

<p>The rugged terrain around the lighthouse and cliffs calls for closed shoes, better suited than beach sandals to walking on uneven ground. Sun protection remains essential during the bathing season, from June to November, since most of the visit happens outdoors with no shade.</p>

<h3>Check the weather before leaving</h3>

<p>The cliffside road in the final kilometres becomes noticeably less pleasant in strong wind or rain, conditions more common on this exposed headland than on Filfila's sheltered beaches. A quick check of the day's forecast before setting off avoids unpleasant surprises.</p>

<h2>Budget and duration to plan for this trip</h2>

<p>The main cost is transport: fuel for a personal vehicle or the rate for a day-long car rental. On top of that comes a possible lunch along the way, in Collo or at an intermediate stop, and a margin for unexpected delays on the winding road. Unlike a boat excursion from Stora, no third-party provider's rate comes into play for Cap Bougaroun itself: the site remains freely accessible by car, with no entry fee.</p>
