<p><strong>El Kala National Park</strong> is worth the trip for a traveller based in Filfila, but it's a full-day excursion rather than a spur-of-the-moment outing: the site lies well beyond the 110 km that already separate Skikda from Annaba, in the El Tarf region, almost at the Tunisian border. Listed as a UNESCO biosphere reserve, it combines freshwater lakes rare along the Algerian coastline with vast cork oak forests, a landscape very different from the sandy beaches of the Skikda coast.</p>

<p>For a traveller who has already enjoyed Filfila's beaches, Stora's waterfront, or a trip to the Guerbès-Senhadja wetland, El Kala is an excursion of a different order: more distant, more nature-focused, and requiring slightly more planning than a simple day trip to Collo or Annaba. This article covers what makes the site special, the real distance to anticipate from the residence, how to combine the visit with a stop in Annaba, and when this excursion genuinely makes sense within a stay based in Filfila.</p>

<h2>What El Kala National Park is</h2>

<p><strong>El Kala National Park</strong>, created to protect a unique set of wetlands and forests on Algeria's eastern coast, covers the El Tarf region, beyond Annaba. Its reputation rests on the rare coexistence of several ecosystems in a single area: freshwater lakes, marshland, coastal dunes and cork oak forests climbing inland.</p>

<h3>The lakes, the heart of the park</h3>

<p>Several freshwater lakes structure the park, surrounded by reed beds and forests that make them favoured stopover points for migratory birds travelling between Europe and Africa according to the seasons. Freshwater lakes this close to the coast remain rare at this scale along the Algerian coastline, which explains the site's biosphere reserve status.</p>

<h3>The cork oak forest</h3>

<p>The cork oak forests covering a large part of the park bring a dense woodland atmosphere, quite different from the more common scrubland and pine groves along the Skikda coast. Cork oak bark, traditionally harvested in the region, gives these forests both ecological and long-established economic value in eastern Algeria.</p>

<h2>Why this trip is not like the others from Filfila</h2>

<p>Unlike <a href="/en/blog/visiter-collo-depuis-skikda">a trip to Collo</a> or an afternoon in <a href="/en/blog/annaba-depuis-skikda">Annaba</a>, reaching El Kala from Filfila means going beyond the already significant distance separating Skikda from Annaba. That's the most important thing to anticipate before setting off on this excursion.</p>

<h3>Annaba, an unavoidable stop</h3>

<p>Annaba is about 110 km from Skikda, already a sizeable drive for a day trip. El Kala lies further still, to the east, adding a good extra hour of driving once past Annaba. Overall, it's best to leave early in the morning from Filfila and treat this as a full-day outing rather than the half-day trips more common closer to the residence.</p>

<h3>Combining the drive with a stop in Annaba</h3>

<p>Most visitors heading to El Kala from Skikda stop in Annaba on the way there or back, splitting up a long drive and fitting in two destinations in a single outing. This mirrors the logic described for a simple visit to Annaba, applied over an even longer distance.</p>

<h2>What to see and do at El Kala</h2>

<p>Once on site, the programme stays essentially nature-focused: watching the lakes, walking along forest trails and, depending on the season, observing migratory birds on the water bodies. This type of outing echoes, on a larger scale, what the <a href="/en/blog/reserve-guerbes-senhadja">Guerbès-Senhadja wetland</a> already offers closer to Filfila.</p>

<h3>Wildlife and bird watching</h3>

<p>The park's lakes attract wading birds, ducks and other waterbirds, with activity often more interesting early in the morning or late in the day. Visitors interested in birdwatching should bring binoculars and keep a quiet approach, the best way to enjoy the site without disturbing the wildlife.</p>

<h3>Forest roads and viewpoints</h3>

<p>The roads crossing the cork oak forest offer several viewpoints over the lakes and surrounding terrain. These are simple, car-accessible stops that don't require extended walking, making them suitable for visitors not used to demanding nature outings.</p>

<h2>Should the visit be organised with a guide</h2>

<p>A guide isn't essential to enjoy the viewpoints reachable by car, but a local escort familiar with the area helps better target access to the lakes and the best periods for wildlife watching. Without a guide, it's recommended to stick to existing routes, check locally on road conditions before setting off, and fill up on fuel before leaving Annaba, as fuel stations become scarcer inside the park.</p>

<h2>When this excursion makes sense</h2>

<p>El Kala suits mainly travellers staying a week or more in Filfila and looking for a nature outing very different from the beaches, rather than a short weekend stay. For a three- or four-day stay, a closer excursion such as <a href="/en/blog/visiter-collo-depuis-skikda">Collo</a> or the Guerbès-Senhadja wetland remains more realistic and leaves more time to enjoy the sea.</p>
