<p>In Skikda, autumn extends the bathing season, which officially runs from <strong>June to November</strong>: October is often still swimmable for regular bathers, while November progressively closes the window. Temperatures stay mild, rain is still occasional early in the season, and accommodation rates drop noticeably compared with July-August.</p>

<p>For an English-speaking traveller or a diaspora member weighing a last swimming window against a more heritage-focused stay, understanding Skikda's autumn climate helps balance weather, quiet and budget. This article details temperatures and the sea month by month from October to November, the concrete advantages of this period, the best-suited activities, and the pitfalls to avoid before fixing dates.</p>

<h2>Skikda's autumn climate: an overview</h2>

<p>Skikda has a <strong>Mediterranean climate</strong> marked by hot, dry summers and mild but rainy winters. Autumn is the transition between these two extremes, running in the opposite direction from spring: summer heat fades gradually, rain becomes more frequent week by week, and the sea, which stored its heat all summer, cools with a lag behind the air.</p>

<h3>October: summer's lingering end</h3>

<p>October is often the most pleasant autumn month in Skikda. Days stay comfortably warm, sunshine still dominates, and the sea keeps much of its summer heat. It is a hinge month where the beach remains a credible option, with crowds already well down from the height of summer.</p>

<h3>November: the shift to a mild winter</h3>

<p>November marks a sharper change. Rain becomes more frequent, temperatures drop noticeably towards month's end, and the sea loses its residual warmth. Swimming becomes secondary for most visitors, but the climate stays mild compared with Europe, leaving plenty of room for daytime outdoor activities.</p>

<h2>Is the sea still swimmable in autumn?</h2>

<p>Skikda's <strong>bathing season</strong> officially runs from June to November, with peak warmth around 26 °C at the height of summer. In autumn, the sea follows a curve opposite to spring: it stays warm in October thanks to summer's stored heat, then cools progressively through November. Many travellers discovering Skikda off-season are surprised by how mild the water is in October, often more pleasant than in June when the sea has only just come out of winter.</p>

<p>That does not mean the coastline loses its appeal once swimming is less comfortable: the beaches of Filfila, Stora, the Grande Plage or Larbi Ben M'hidi (formerly Jeanne d'Arc) remain lovely to walk along or photograph in November, simply without the extended swimming that defines summer.</p>
