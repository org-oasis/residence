<p>For a <strong>family in Skikda</strong>, the budget splits across four categories: accommodation, food, transport and activities. A family of 4 to 6 staying in an F2 or F3 with a fully equipped kitchen, enjoying Filfila's free beaches, spends most of its budget on accommodation, from {{TIER_F2_EUR}} to {{TIER_F3_EUR}} € per night outside peak season depending on the chosen unit, with the other categories staying noticeably lighter.</p>

<p>Unlike a couple's trip, a family budget needs to account for the unit's real capacity, the cost of guests beyond that capacity, and activities that keep children busy without blowing the budget. This guide breaks down each spending category specific to a family stay, compares the formats available at Résidence Oasis, and offers concrete budget profiles for planning a trip to Filfila.</p>

<h2>The spending categories of a family stay in Skikda</h2>

<p>A family trip is structured differently from a couple's trip: the nightly cost is split across more people, but choosing the right apartment format and factoring in any surcharge for guests beyond base capacity become decisive variables.</p>

<h3>Accommodation: the central but shared category</h3>

<p>Unlike a couple, who carries the full nightly rate alone, a family of 4, 6 or 8 splits that same cost across several people, lowering the per-person cost even though the apartment's nightly rate stays the same.</p>

<h3>Extra guests: a category to plan for</h3>

<p>Beyond a unit's base capacity, up to 2 extra guests can be added per apartment, for a surcharge per night per person. For a larger family slightly over an F2's or F3's capacity, this surcharge is usually more economical than booking a second unit.</p>

<h3>Food and activities: flexible categories with children</h3>

<p>With children, the food and activities budget depends heavily on family choices: cooking on site sharply limits food spending, while the wilaya's free beaches make it possible to build a rich program without piling up paid outings.</p>

<h3>Which format for which family size</h3>

<p>An F2, built for 4 people, suits a couple with two children. A family of 5 or 6 can choose an F2 with the extra-guest surcharge, or go straight for the F3 with private terrace, built for 8 people and more comfortable for a larger or multi-generational group.</p>

<h3>The effect of the long-stay discount</h3>

<p>A week corresponds exactly to the threshold at which a 10% discount applies to the total rate. For a family planning a 7-night stay or longer rather than a short weekend, this discount directly eases the heaviest line of the family budget, with no extra steps required.</p>

<h2>Family food budget: the value of an equipped kitchen</h2>

<p>The food category often weighs more heavily with children, given how often they eat and the dietary needs specific to younger kids. The residence's <a href="/en/apartments">available studios and F2/F3 units</a> all come with a fully equipped kitchen (stove, oven, fridge, coffee maker), a major asset for a family.</p>

<h3>Cooking for the whole family</h3>

<p>Preparing breakfasts and some meals yourselves sharply cuts food spending compared to eating out every day, a gap that grows with the number of people at the table. The markets of Filfila and downtown Skikda provide fresh produce, including fish, suited to all ages.</p>

<h3>Eating out as a family</h3>

<p>Eating out as a family remains affordable by European standards, especially for local cuisine and seafood. Booking an occasional restaurant outing, notably around Stora which is known for fresh fish, nicely rounds out a stay where most meals are prepared on site.</p>

<h2>Transport budget for a car-free family</h2>

<p>The transport category stays modest for a family staying near the sea and centering their trip on Filfila. Résidence Oasis, 700 m from the beach in the Jeanne d'Arc neighborhood, lets you reach the coast on foot for most daily outings, a real advantage with young children and strollers.</p>

<ul>
  <li>Daily trips to the beach: on foot, at no cost</li>
  <li>Trips to Stora or downtown Skikda: local transport or taxi, a moderate budget</li>
  <li>Longer excursions (Collo, Constantine, Cap Bougaroun): car rental or long-distance taxi, a one-off category to budget separately, especially with several children</li>
  <li>Arrival transfer: to plan for depending on how you travel to Skikda</li>
</ul>

<h2>Kids' activities budget: relying on free beaches</h2>

<p>The wilaya of Skikda offers a notable budget advantage for families: beaches, the main daily activity with children, are entirely free. Filfila, Stora, Larbi Ben M'hidi and Les Calatines charge no entry fee at all.</p>

<h3>Free daily activities</h3>

<p>Swimming, sandcastles, walks along the coast and watching the fishing boats at Stora keep children busy without any extra spending. These activities cover most of a week-long family program.</p>

<h3>Occasional paid outings</h3>

<p>A waterpark or a boat trip from Stora remain paid options to budget separately, as a complement to the free program rather than a replacement. It's best to plan no more than one or two such outings per week to keep the budget under control.</p>

<h2>Common mistakes to avoid on the family budget</h2>

<ul>
  <li>Booking an F2 for 6 people without planning for the extra-guest surcharge</li>
  <li>Underestimating the food budget by assuming children eat little, when meal frequency matters more than portion size</li>
  <li>Stacking too many paid outings (waterpark, excursions) until the activities budget gets out of hand</li>
  <li>Booking in peak season without comparing with June or September, often more affordable for a family</li>
  <li>Forgetting to factor transport for longer excursions into the overall budget from the start of planning</li>
</ul>
