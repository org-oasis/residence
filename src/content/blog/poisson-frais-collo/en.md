<p><strong>Fresh fish in Collo</strong> is best eaten at the small restaurants of the village, over an hour's drive from Filfila, on the western coast of the Skikda region. The choice there is more modest than in Stora, but the fish comes straight from local fishing, in a village atmosphere still largely untouched by beach tourism.</p>

<p>For a traveller based in Filfila, Collo represents a different kind of outing from those focused on the beach or heritage: a food-and-landscape excursion to a wilder stretch of coastline, where daily life is still shaped by fishing rather than by the tourist calendar. This article details what's on the plate in Collo, how to plan the trip from Filfila, and how it compares to the region's other fish addresses.</p>

<h2>Collo, a still-authentic fishing village</h2>

<p>Collo lies west of the Skikda region, beyond Stora, on a stretch of coast that is more rugged and less developed than the Filfila area. The village keeps up a daily fishing activity that directly supplies the few restaurants near the harbour, with no long logistics chain between the boat and the plate.</p>

<h3>A more limited offer than Stora</h3>

<p>Unlike <a href="/en/blog/poisson-frais-stora">Stora, the region's top address for fresh fish</a>, Collo offers a smaller number of restaurants. That's exactly what makes it a different kind of address: fewer choices, but an atmosphere closer to the daily life of fishermen, far from the summer crowds of the busier areas.</p>

<h3>What local fishing brings to the table</h3>

<p>As along the rest of the region's coast, fishing in Collo varies by season. Sardine, sea bream, red mullet, grouper and octopus come up regularly on the tables, rounded out by squid or shrimp depending on the day's catch. The menu at Collo's small restaurants therefore naturally changes from one day to the next, depending on what the boats brought back that morning.</p>

<h2>How to get to Collo from Filfila</h2>

<p>From the Jeanne d'Arc district in Filfila, allow over an hour's drive to reach Collo, along the coastal road that follows a good stretch of the region's western coastline. The drive itself is pleasant, with sea views along the way, but requires careful driving on a winding coastal road.</p>

<h3>A vehicle is essential</h3>

<p>No direct, regular public transport serves Collo from Filfila. A personal car, a day rental, or a taxi booked in advance remain the three realistic options for planning the trip without being tied to fixed schedules.</p>

<h2>Planning a food day in Collo</h2>

<p>A trip to Collo is best built around a fish lunch rather than treated as a quick stop. Since the round trip already takes up a good part of the day, it's best to plan an early departure, a stop in the village to watch the harbour's activity in late morning, then lunch before heading back.</p>

<h3>When to go to make the most of Collo</h3>

<p>Collo is worth visiting year-round, with the bathing season running from June to November as along the rest of the region's coast. Outside July-August, the roads are less busy and restaurant tables are easier to find without advance booking. The high season brings more life, but also a bit more waiting at some restaurants, particularly at weekends.</p>

<h2>Beyond the meal: Collo's coastline</h2>

<p>The fish lunch is often just part of the programme. Collo sits on a coastline that is more rugged and less developed than Filfila or Stora, which allows the trip to be extended with a walk along the coast or a stop at small coves accessible from the road. This meal-and-scenery combination largely justifies the longer drive from Filfila.</p>

<h3>A village pace rather than a tourist zone</h3>

<p>Unlike Filfila, which is shaped around welcoming holidaymakers, Collo keeps a village identity where fishing remains a daily activity rather than simply a tourist attraction. Shops, schedules and the general atmosphere follow this pace, which asks for a bit of flexibility from visitors used to areas more organised for beach tourism.</p>

<h2>Combining Collo with other outings during the stay</h2>

<p>Since the trip to Collo already takes up a good part of the day, it isn't advisable to combine it with another distant excursion the same day. For a week-long stay in Filfila, it's better to spread Collo and a trip to Stora over separate days, keeping the other days for the beach and activities in the Jeanne d'Arc district.
