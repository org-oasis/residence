<p>Visiting <strong>Chetaïbi from Skikda</strong> requires a car and a bit of planning: allow first for the 20 km between Filfila and Skikda town centre, then a good extra hour along the coastal road toward Annaba to reach this fishing village that has largely stayed off the mass-tourism map. No regular public transport line serves Chetaïbi directly, making a personal or rented vehicle the obligatory starting point for the trip.</p>

<p>For a traveller based in Filfila who has already explored the family beaches and Stora harbour, Chetaïbi offers a different kind of outing: rocky coves, small sheltered bays and the atmosphere of a maritime village that has changed little. This article details the route from the residence, what there is to see and do on site, the best time to go, and how to plan the day without surprises.</p>

<h2>Where Chetaïbi sits relative to Skikda and Filfila</h2>

<p>Chetaïbi is a coastal village located east of the Skikda region, on the road that follows the coastline toward Annaba. Unlike Collo or Cap Bougaroun, which lie west of Skikda, Chetaïbi is reached by crossing the town of Skikda and continuing east, making it an outing in the opposite direction from Collo.</p>

<h3>A fishing village off the main tourist routes</h3>

<p>The village still largely lives off fishing, with a small harbour and traditional boats rather than developed seaside infrastructure. The coves lining the coast around Chetaïbi are cut into a more rugged terrain than Filfila's, which limits road access to some of them and preserves their wild character.</p>

<h3>Route from Filfila</h3>

<p>From the Jeanne d'Arc district, the route runs through Skikda town centre (20 km) before joining the coastal road heading east. The road passes through several intermediate towns and follows the coastline for a good part of the journey, making the drive itself enjoyable for lovers of seaside scenery.</p>

<h2>How to get to Chetaïbi</h2>

<p>A car remains the only practical way to reach Chetaïbi comfortably. No direct, regular public transport line connects Filfila or Skikda to the village, and any connections would considerably lengthen the trip without any guarantee of reliable timing.</p>

<h3>Via the coastal road through Skikda</h3>

<p>After the 20 km separating Filfila from Skikda town centre, the road continues east for a good extra hour, depending on traffic and driving conditions around the town. The last few kilometres before Chetaïbi follow a winding route, typical of the region's coastal roads, which calls for attentive driving but remains manageable without particular difficulty.</p>

<h3>Road conditions and time of year</h3>

<p>In summer, traffic around Skikda and on the coastal road can noticeably lengthen the trip, particularly in late morning and late afternoon. Leaving early avoids most of this congestion and leaves more of the day to enjoy on site, with the coves and village particularly pleasant in morning light.</p>

<h2>What to see and do in Chetaïbi</h2>

<p>Chetaïbi's main appeal lies in its jagged coastline, very different from Filfila's continuous sandy beaches, and in the atmosphere of a village that still lives off the sea day to day.</p>

<h3>Coves and small sheltered bays</h3>

<p>The rocky coves dotting the coast around Chetaïbi generally offer calmer, clearer water than busier beach areas, well suited to swimming and snorkelling during the season that runs from June to November. Some are reachable only on foot from the nearest stopping points, which naturally limits crowds even in the height of summer.</p>

<h3>The fishing harbour and village</h3>

<p>Chetaïbi's small harbour stays animated by the comings and goings of traditional fishing boats. Strolling along the quay, watching the boats come back in, or simply enjoying the village's calm is part of the experience, in a register more authentic and less tourist-oriented than Stora.</p>

<h3>Informal water activities</h3>

<p>Beyond swimming, the clear waters of the coves suit snorkelling well for travellers who bring their own gear. Local rental options remain limited, unlike Filfila where beach equipment is more readily available, which is reason enough to bring a mask and fins before setting off.</p>

<h2>A coastline preserved by its isolation</h2>

<p>Chetaïbi's relative distance from the region's main tourist routes largely explains the still-preserved state of its coastline. The least accessible coves by car keep particularly clear water and little-disturbed coastal vegetation, a sharp contrast with Filfila's busiest areas in high summer. This isolation has a flip side, though: shops, tourist signage and emergency services are less present than in a developed seaside area, which is reason enough to set off with water, a full tank of fuel and, ideally, not to go alone outside peak visiting hours.</p>

<h3>A destination that changes with the seasons</h3>

<p>Like the rest of the region's coastline, Chetaïbi experiences a sharp contrast between summer, part of the bathing season running from June to November, and the rest of the year. Outside that period, the village returns to its usual fishing rhythm, with far fewer visitors but also near-deserted coves for travellers looking above all for calm rather than swimming.</p>
