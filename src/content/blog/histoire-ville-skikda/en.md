<p>Skikda was called <strong>Rusicade</strong> in antiquity, a trading post and later a Roman port on the Mediterranean. Renamed Philippeville by French colonization in the 19th century, it took back its current name at Algeria's independence in 1962. Three names, three eras, for the same port city about 20 km from Filfila.</p>

<p>For a traveler on a beach holiday at Résidence Oasis, knowing these key stages gives a different meaning to a simple trip into town: the port, the street layout, certain buildings tell a story far longer than the tourist season alone. This article traces the main periods that shaped Skikda, from its ancient origins to its identity today, and suggests ways to see concrete traces of it during a stay.</p>

<h2>Rusicade, the ancient origins of Skikda</h2>

<p>Before it was called Skikda, the city already existed under the name <strong>Rusicade</strong>, a trading post of Punic origin established on this stretch of Mediterranean coast for its natural harbor qualities. This early settlement already benefited from the same strategic position that makes Skikda an active port today: a natural shelter on the sea, at the hinge between the coastline and the mountainous hinterland of eastern Algeria.</p>

<h3>Rusicade under the Roman Empire</h3>

<p>As the Roman Empire expanded across North Africa, Rusicade developed as a secondary port serving Cirta, present-day Constantine, then an important regional capital. This relationship between the coastal port and the major inland city foreshadows, in a way, the link that still exists today between Skikda and Constantine, now connected by a road of about 100 km used by travelers and trade alike.</p>

<h3>Ancient heritage scattered across the region</h3>

<p>The most visible traces of this ancient period are not confined to today's city center: they are scattered across several sites in the wilaya, evidence of long, continuous occupation of this stretch of coast well before the arrival of Islam and later French colonization.</p>

<h2>From Rusicade to Philippeville: the colonial period</h2>

<p>After centuries marked by Muslim dynasties and then the Ottoman period, the city reaches a major turning point with French colonization in the 19th century. The colonial administration renames the city <strong>Philippeville</strong>, in honor of King Louis-Philippe, and undertakes a deep urban restructuring of the port site.</p>

<h3>A city reshaped by colonial urban planning</h3>

<p>It is during this period that the city takes on much of the appearance it still has today in its historic center: a regular street grid, buildings of European architecture around the port, and an urban expansion designed for the port and commercial activity of the colonial era. This layout remains readable for anyone walking through the city center today.</p>

<h3>The port, engine of the colonial city</h3>

<p>The development of Philippeville relies heavily on its port, which becomes a major export point for resources from the Constantine hinterland. This port vocation, inherited directly from the era of Rusicade, structures the local economy throughout the colonial period and continues to mark the city's identity today.</p>

<h2>1962: the return to the name Skikda</h2>

<p>Algeria's independence, in <strong>1962</strong>, marks a new turning point: the city drops the colonial name of Philippeville and takes back a name close to its original one, Skikda. This change is part of a broader movement to reclaim place names across the country after the colonial period.</p>

<h3>A city rebuilt after independence</h3>

<p>The decades following independence see Skikda develop as a major industrial and port hub of eastern Algeria, while retaining its role as a coastal city valued for its beaches, including those of Stora and Filfila. This dual identity, industrial and seaside, still characterizes the wilaya today.</p>

<h2>What remains visible of this history today</h2>

<p>For a visitor curious about history during their stay, several elements make it possible to observe these different layers concretely:</p>

<ul>
  <li>The colonial architecture of certain city-center neighborhoods, inherited from the Philippeville era</li>
  <li>The regular street layout of the historic center, drawn up during that same period</li>
  <li>The port itself, still active, which extends a port vocation dating back to antiquity</li>
  <li>Ancient traces scattered across the wilaya, evidence of the era of Rusicade</li>
</ul>

<h2>Common misconceptions about Skikda's history</h2>

<ul>
  <li>Confusing Rusicade, the ancient name, with Philippeville, which belongs to a much more recent and different period</li>
  <li>Assuming the city's heritage is limited to the tourist seafront, when the historic center holds most of the visible traces</li>
