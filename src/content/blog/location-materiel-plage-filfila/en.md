<p>In <strong>Filfila</strong>, supervised beaches such as Larbi Ben M'hidi beach (formerly Jeanne d'Arc), roughly 7 km long, host seasonal vendors renting parasols, sunloungers and sometimes kayaks throughout the swimming season, from June to November. Rental usually happens on site, with no booking needed, which saves you from traveling with bulky equipment for a short stay.</p>

<p>For a traveler planning a stay at Residence Oasis, the question often comes up: should you bring your own beach gear or rely on local rental? This article reviews the actual supply available in Filfila and along the Skikda coast, the types of equipment you can find, a rough price range, and good habits for organizing beach days without any surprise.</p>

<h2>What beach equipment can you rent in Filfila</h2>

<p>The rental supply varies by beach and by time of year, but a few pieces of equipment show up consistently on the busiest beaches in the Skikda region.</p>

<h3>Parasols and sunloungers, the most common gear</h3>

<p>The parasol and sunlounger pair is the most widespread offering among seasonal vendors. Set up in rows on the sand near supervised areas, they let you settle in for the day without carrying your own equipment from your accommodation. Rental is usually by the day or half-day, paid directly on site in cash.</p>

<h3>Kayaks, pedal boats and guided water activities</h3>

<p>On some beaches, especially in high season, vendors also offer kayaks, pedal boats and sometimes guided paddleboard outings. This offering is less consistent than parasols and sunloungers and depends heavily on which vendor is present that day. For a water activity planned in advance rather than improvised on site, a <a href="/en/blog/excursion-bateau-skikda">boat excursion along the Skikda coast</a> is a more organized alternative.</p>

<h3>Small gear for children</h3>

<p>Floats, armbands and small beach toys are also available to rent or buy from vendors on the beaches most popular with families. For a stay with young children, it's still safer to bring your own certified armbands rather than rely solely on local supply, whose quality can vary.</p>

<h2>Where to find the widest choice along the Skikda coast</h2>

<p>Not every beach in the region offers the same level of supply. The density of vendors fairly logically follows each site's tourist footfall.</p>

<h3>Filfila's beaches, the closest supply to the residence</h3>

<p>In Filfila, Larbi Ben M'hidi beach holds most of the region's rental supply, due to its length and popularity. It's also the most convenient beach for stays at Residence Oasis, given its immediate proximity. To compare the area's different options, <a href="/en/blog/meilleure-plage-filfila">a dedicated article on Filfila's best beaches</a> details each site's particularities.</p>

<h3>Stora and the region's other beaches</h3>

<p>In Stora, the fishing-port atmosphere takes precedence over classic rental supply, even if a few vendors offer equipment there in high season. On more distant beaches like Les Calatines or Grande Plage, rental supply exists but is generally less dense than in Filfila. It's therefore worth checking on site on arrival rather than assuming the same supply everywhere.</p>

<h2>Rough prices and how rental works</h2>

<p>Rental prices for beach equipment vary by season, vendor and how busy the day is. They are usually posted or announced directly at the stand, with no need to book ahead.</p>

<h3>A simple system, paid in cash</h3>

<p>Rental is almost always paid in cash, in Algerian dinars, directly to the vendor on the beach. It's therefore worth carrying a few small notes before heading to the beach, on top of your budget for meals or excursions. To plan for this, <a href="/en/blog/cash-carte-bancaire-skikda">an article on paying in cash or by card in Skikda</a> covers local habits in more detail.</p>

<h3>High season vs. off season</h3>

<p>In July-August, demand is highest and the choice of spots narrows by late morning on the busiest beaches. Outside high season, between October and May, vendors are far less present or even absent on some beaches, which makes it more useful to bring your own lightweight equipment to enjoy the coast outside the warmest months.</p>

<h2>Should you bring your own gear or rent on site</h2>

<p>The choice mostly depends on the length of the stay, the time of year and the type of activity you're after.</p>

<h3>Advantages of renting on site</h3>

<ul>
  <li>No bulky equipment to carry from your home country</li>
  <li>Full flexibility: rental by the day, with no commitment</li>
  <li>Easy access to one-off equipment like a kayak, without buying anything</li>
  <li>Well suited to a short stay or a single beach day</li>
</ul>

<h3>Advantages of bringing your own equipment</h3>

<ul>
  <li>Guaranteed availability even outside high season, when vendors are scarcer</li>
  <li>Familiar, suitable equipment, especially for children's safety (certified armbands)</li>
  <li>Savings over a longer stay with several consecutive beach days</li>
  <li>More freedom on timing, without depending on stand opening hours</li>
</ul>
