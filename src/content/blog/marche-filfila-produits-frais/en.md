<p>In Filfila, fresh produce is best bought first at the local shops in the Jeanne d'Arc neighborhood for everyday needs, then at Stora for fish straight off the boats, and finally in downtown Skikda, about 20 km away, for a wider choice of fruit and vegetables or the atmosphere of a traditional market. These three options cover most needs of a beach stay, without relying solely on restaurants.</p>

<p>For a traveler staying in an equipped apartment who wants to cook some or all of their meals, knowing where to find quality fresh produce changes the organization of the stay considerably. This article covers the options available in and around Filfila, the best way to stock up on fresh fish in Stora, what sets downtown Skikda apart for more complete shopping, and a few practical pointers to plan ahead.</p>

<h2>Local shops in Filfila</h2>

<p>The Jeanne d'Arc neighborhood, where Résidence Oasis is located, has local shops that cover the everyday needs of a stay: fruit, vegetables, basic groceries, and daily essentials. This offering is designed for convenience rather than variety, which suits quick shopping trips between beach days without a long journey.</p>

<h3>What you'll easily find on site</h3>

<ul>
  <li>Seasonal fruit and vegetables, in quantities sufficient for daily needs</li>
  <li>Basic groceries (bread, eggs, dairy, drinks)</li>
  <li>A few additional fresh items depending on the shop</li>
</ul>

<h3>What's sometimes missing at neighborhood scale</h3>

<p>For a wider choice of fruit and vegetables, larger quantities, or more specific products, Filfila's local shops quickly reach their limits. That's where downtown Skikda or an occasional trip to Stora make sense, as a complement rather than a substitute for everyday shopping.</p>

<h2>Stora, the reference for fresh fish</h2>

<p>The <a href="/en/blog/poisson-frais-stora">port of Stora</a>, about twenty minutes from Filfila, remains the best address in the whole province for buying fresh fish directly. Fishing boats return in the late morning and early afternoon, supplying both the port's restaurants and individuals coming to buy fish to cook in their apartment.</p>

<h3>How buying fish at Stora works</h3>

<p>Vendors sell the day's catch depending on what came in, generally right on the quay or at small stalls near the boats. It's worth arriving relatively early after the boats return to benefit from the widest choice, before the best pieces go to the port's restaurants or the first buyers.</p>

<h3>Cooking Stora's fish at Résidence Oasis</h3>

<p>Once the fish is bought, the apartment's <a href="/en/blog/appartement-cuisine-equipee-skikda">equipped kitchen</a> makes it simple to prepare — grilled or in a sauce, depending on preference. It's an economical and flavorful option, much appreciated by travelers who want to mix restaurant meals with home cooking during their stay.</p>

<h2>Downtown Skikda for a fuller selection</h2>

<p>Downtown Skikda, about 20 km from Filfila, holds the province's widest commercial offering: fruit and vegetable markets, better-stocked grocery stores, and specialty shops. It's the destination to favor for more complete shopping, particularly ahead of a multi-day stay or for a larger family.</p>

<h3>The atmosphere of a traditional Algerian market</h3>

<p>Beyond the practical side, a trip to downtown Skikda's market is also an experience in itself: colorful stalls, exchanges with vendors, and seasonal produce on display. It's an activity that nicely complements a beach stay, for travelers curious to step outside the beach-and-apartment routine.</p>

<h2>Organizing shopping according to the length of stay</h2>

<p>Shopping strategy largely depends on the length of the stay. For a short stay of a few days, Filfila's local shops are usually enough, possibly supplemented by a fish trip to Stora. For a longer stay, an occasional trip downtown to Skikda lets you stock up and add more variety to your produce.</p>

<h3>Seasonal fruit and vegetables in summer</h3>

<p>The summer period, from June to September, is when summer fruit is at its peak on the stalls: watermelon, melon, grapes, and figs are among the season's classics. This period coincides with the busiest swimming season, which keeps commercial activity in Filfila more active than in the low season.</p>

<h3>Bring cash for purchases</h3>

<p>Local shops and traditional markets run very largely on cash, in Algerian dinars. It's recommended to carry enough cash for buying fresh produce, whether in Filfila, Stora, or downtown Skikda.</p>

<h2>Spices, herbs, and pantry items</h2>

<p>Beyond fruit, vegetables, and fish, spices and fresh herbs are easy to find both in Filfila and downtown Skikda. Coriander, parsley, fresh mint for tea, along with the staple spices of Algerian cooking (cumin, paprika, ras el hanout), are readily available at local shops as well as the better-stocked downtown markets. These pantry items make it easy to season meals prepared in the apartment, including fish grilled with produce bought at Stora.</p>

<h3>Shopping as a family</h3>

<p>For a family staying at Résidence Oasis, planning shopping ahead makes daily life much easier: a first stop at Filfila's local shops on arrival for staples, then an occasional trip to Stora for fish or downtown Skikda if the stay runs longer than a week. This two-step approach avoids repeated back-and-forth trips and leaves more time for the beach, while keeping the kitchen well stocked thanks to the apartment's full equipment.</p>

<h2>Common mistakes to avoid</h2>

<p>A few mistakes come up regularly among travelers discovering how to organize shopping in Filfila for the first time. Anticipating them makes managing the stay much simpler.</p>

<ul>
  <li>Waiting until the last day of the stay to look for fresh produce, when local shops cover everyday needs from the moment you arrive</li>
  <li>Heading to Stora late in the afternoon expecting the best fish selection, when boats return in the late morning</li>
  <li>Planning to pay only by card, when cash remains largely dominant for this type of purchase</li>
  <li>Underestimating the travel time downtown for a fuller shop, about 20 km from Filfila</li>
