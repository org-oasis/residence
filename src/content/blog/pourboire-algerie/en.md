<p>In Algeria, <strong>tipping</strong> is never mandatory: unlike some countries where service work is poorly paid, it's a free gesture rather than a strict social rule. At a restaurant, rounding up the bill or leaving the equivalent of a few hundred dinars is appreciated without being expected; at the hammam, a small tip handed over after a massage is the most common practice. This guide covers the customs by context for a stress-free stay in Skikda and Filfila.</p>

<p>For a French-speaking traveller, an expatriate or a member of the diaspora visiting, the question of tipping often comes up, precisely because customs differ from one country to another and no official scale exists in Algeria. Not knowing what to do can create needless unease, whether from fear of offending by leaving nothing, or of appearing out of place by giving too much. This article covers the actual customs observed in Skikda and the Filfila region, context by context, so you can approach your stay without that kind of hesitation.</p>

<h2>The general principle of tipping in Algeria</h2>

<p>The starting point to remember is simple: <strong>tipping remains optional</strong> everywhere in Algeria, whatever the service involved. There is no officially recommended percentage, nor an automatic "service" line added to a bill. The practice of tipping developed through custom rather than rule, and varies noticeably by context, type of establishment and region.</p>

<h3>A gesture of satisfaction, not a contractual obligation</h3>

<p>Locally, a tip is seen as a mark of satisfaction with a service judged particularly good, not as part of the price. A traveller who leaves nothing does nothing wrong; conversely, a small gesture for a service rendered with care is always well received, without ever being openly demanded in most situations.</p>

<h3>Why this question mostly comes up for foreign visitors</h3>

<p>Residents of the Skikda region and Algerians in general are naturally familiar with these unwritten customs. It's mainly visitors from abroad, used to different practices (a 10-15% tip common in some countries), who wonder about it before or during their stay, lacking a clear reference point.</p>

<h2>Tipping at restaurants</h2>

<p>The restaurant is the most common context where the question of tipping arises during a stay in Skikda, whether at a town-centre establishment or one of the renowned fresh fish restaurants around Stora.</p>

<h3>Rounding up the bill, the most common practice</h3>

<p>The most widespread practice is simply to round the bill up to the next amount, or leave the equivalent of a few hundred dinars in small change if the service was satisfactory. It's never a percentage calculated on the total, as might be customary elsewhere.</p>

<h3>At tourist-oriented seaside restaurants</h3>

<p>At establishments more used to a foreign clientele, particularly around the busiest areas during the swimming season (June to November), a light tip is slightly more consistently appreciated, without this changing the optional nature of the gesture. Staff remain courteous and professional regardless of whether a tip is left.</p>

<h2>Tipping for taxis and transport</h2>

<p>For a taxi ride in Skikda or between Filfila and the town centre, the question of tipping comes up differently than at a restaurant, since the fare is often negotiated or known in advance.</p>

<h3>Rounding up the fare rather than calculating a percentage</h3>

<p>The custom is to round the fare up to the next amount, particularly when the driver helps load luggage or takes on a somewhat longer route with no extra charge announced. No percentage is expected, and a fare settled at the price agreed in advance requires no addition at all.</p>

<h3>The special case of full-day excursions</h3>

<p>For a full-day excursion toward Collo, Cap Bougaroun, or a longer outing with a driver, a small extra gesture at the end of the day is appreciated if the service went beyond the initial scope (multiple photo stops, local advice, flexibility on timing), without this becoming a systematic rule.</p>

<h2>Tipping at the hammam and for wellness services</h2>

<p>The traditional hammam is arguably the context where tipping is most solidly rooted in local custom, without being formally imposed.</p>

<h3>After a scrub or massage</h3>

<p>The hammam fee covers access and the basic service, paid at the counter on entry or exit. The tip, meanwhile, is generally handed directly to the person who performed the scrub or massage, as a sign of appreciation for the quality of that day's service.</p>

<h3>A free amount, with no imposed scale</h3>

<p>There is no strict reference amount: everyone adjusts according to their satisfaction and budget. What matters most is understanding that this gesture is separate from the hammam's listed price, and it never affects the quality of welcome given to the next visitors.</p>

<h2>Tipping at markets and souks</h2>

<p>In Skikda's traditional souks and markets, the question of tipping rarely comes up in the same way as at a restaurant or hammam, since the commercial relationship there is more direct.</p>

<h3>Negotiation rather than tipping</h3>

<p>At a market, local custom favours negotiating the price rather than adding a tip after purchase. A vendor satisfied with the exchange generally expects nothing beyond the agreed price, and offering a tip in this context can sometimes create an unusual situation for them.</p>

<h3>Porters or occasional helpers</h3>

<p>If someone spontaneously helps carry purchases or points you toward a particular stall, a small gesture remains appreciated, on the same principle as elsewhere: optional, proportionate, and never negotiated in advance.</p>

<h2>What to do if in doubt</h2>

<p>When in doubt, the simplest approach is to observe local habits or ask your host directly, whether that's a property owner, a restaurateur, or a driver. The vast majority of situations require no particular anticipation: tipping remains a secondary part of the overall experience of a stay in Skikda, never a source of trouble if you stay courteous and attentive.</p>
