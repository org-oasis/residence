<p>The most reliable health reflex during a stay in Filfila is the neighbourhood pharmacy for minor issues, and the public hospital in Skikda, about 20 km away, for anything beyond a simple consultation. In between, general practice offices and a few private clinics cover most everyday needs of a traveller on holiday.</p>

<p>A health issue never gives warning, and knowing in advance where to turn saves precious time in the middle of the night or in the middle of a beach day. This article details the care options available around Résidence Oasis, what belongs to the pharmacy, the doctor or the hospital, and the good habits to adopt from arrival for a worry-free stay in Filfila.</p>

<h2>Levels of care available around Filfila</h2>

<p>As in most seaside areas of the Skikda wilaya, the care offer around Filfila is organized across several levels, from the simplest to the most complete. Understanding this hierarchy before even needing it avoids rushing to the hospital for something a pharmacy can solve, or the opposite.</p>

<h3>The pharmacy, the first local reflex</h3>

<p>For sunburn, a small cut, a headache or mild digestive trouble after a hearty meal, the pharmacy remains the fastest entry point. Several pharmacies are located in the Jeanne d'Arc neighbourhood and along the road connecting Filfila to downtown Skikda. Staff generally refer to a doctor or an emergency service if the case goes beyond counter advice.</p>

<h3>The general practice office</h3>

<p>For a consultation that needs medical advice without being an emergency (persistent fever, a growing pain, renewing a treatment), a general practice office is the next step. The offer remains denser in Skikda than in Filfila itself, which often means a short trip into town for this type of scheduled consultation.</p>

<h3>The hospital and private clinics</h3>

<p>For anything that qualifies as a real emergency (trauma, acute pain, a serious feeling unwell) or an examination only a hospital facility can run, the wilaya's public hospital in Skikda remains the reference. Private clinics complement the offer for certain scheduled procedures, often with shorter waiting times but a cost borne by the patient, reimbursable depending on the terms of any travel insurance taken out.</p>

<h2>What to do in a medical emergency</h2>

<p>A medical emergency calls for going straight to the point rather than looking for a precise address at the last moment. In Algeria, the number to dial for a medical emergency is <strong>14</strong>, civil protection, which handles both on-site response and ambulance transport to the appropriate hospital facility. Police remain reachable at 17 and the national gendarmerie at 1055 for anything related to security rather than health. For the full detail of these numbers and the reflexes to have in Algeria, the article on <a href="/en/blog/numeros-urgence-skikda">emergency numbers in Skikda</a> covers every case.</p>

<h3>Notifying the residence in parallel</h3>

<p>Alongside calling for help, notifying the Résidence Oasis team by WhatsApp speeds up the response: the exact address of the accommodation in Filfila, parking access and local landmarks aren't always obvious to an ambulance unfamiliar with the neighbourhood. A host on site can also guide responders by phone or welcome them directly at the residence's entrance.</p>

<h3>Keeping useful numbers accessible before you need them</h3>

<p>Saving Algerian emergency numbers on your phone right on arrival, before any real need arises, avoids searching for them under pressure with a sometimes unstable internet connection. Keeping a copy of your passport, your travel insurance and the residence's contact details somewhere easily accessible, including offline, makes any unexpected step much easier during the stay.</p>

<h2>On-duty pharmacies in Filfila</h2>

<p>Outside regular opening hours, an on-duty system rotates between several pharmacies in the area, following a schedule that changes regularly from one week to the next. There's no fixed address to memorize in advance: the most reliable approach is to ask the residence team directly, right on arrival, which pharmacy is on duty that specific day. The dedicated article on <a href="/en/blog/pharmacie-garde-filfila">on-duty pharmacies in Filfila</a> details this rotation system and how to find your way quickly in case of need in the evening or on weekends.</p>

<h2>Travelling with a chronic condition or specific needs</h2>

<p>A traveller on a regular treatment should plan a sufficient supply for the whole stay, ideally with a few extra days' margin in case of a delayed return. Keeping medication in its original packaging with the leaflet makes any check or local renewal easier with a doctor should the supply run short. A medical summary written in French, mentioning current treatments and any allergies, greatly simplifies an unexpected consultation, especially if the traveller doesn't speak Arabic.</p>

<h3>The particular case of families with young children</h3>

<p>For a family stay, packing a small first-aid kit (a fever reducer suited to the child's weight, plasters, oral rehydration solution) reduces the need to rush out in the middle of the night for a minor issue. Seaside activities multiply small mishaps (jellyfish stings, sunburn, scrapes), already covered in more detail in the article on <a href="/en/blog/precautions-sanitaires-skikda-ete">health precautions to take in Skikda in summer</a>. The residence's <a href="/en/apartments">available studios and F2/F3 units</a>, all air-conditioned, offer a comfortable setting to let a feverish child rest in the shade while waiting for medical advice if needed.</p>

<h3>The case of visitors coming from abroad</h3>

<p>A visitor coming from abroad should check, before departure, that their travel insurance does cover medical costs in Algeria, including a possible medical evacuation. Public facilities charge rates far lower than in many European countries, but adequate coverage still avoids having to advance large sums or handle complicated paperwork while recovering. This topic is covered in the article on <a href="/en/blog/assurance-voyage-algerie">travel insurance for Algeria</a>.</p>

<h2>Plan ahead rather than deal with it on the spot</h2>

<p>The vast majority of stays in Filfila go by without the slightest health incident, and the beach, sun and relaxation remain the main program from June to November, the swimming season on the Skikda coast. But a few minutes of preparation before departure, numbers saved, insurance checked, a small first-aid kit, are enough to turn an unexpected issue into a brief parenthesis rather than a source of stress for the rest of the holiday.</p>
