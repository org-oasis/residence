<p><strong>Three national emergency numbers</strong> cover Skikda and its surroundings, just like the rest of Algeria: police on <strong>17</strong>, civil protection on <strong>14</strong> (fire, medical emergencies, road accidents), and the national gendarmerie on <strong>1055</strong>. These numbers are free, reachable 24/7 from a mobile or landline, and require no special area code for the Skikda province or the municipality of Filfila.</p>

<p>For a French-speaking traveller, an expatriate, or a diaspora member preparing a stay in Skikda, knowing these numbers before departure is basic common sense, on par with booking accommodation or packing a beach bag. This article details the national numbers that apply in Skikda, clarifies when to call which one, covers medical emergencies and duty pharmacies, and gives practical tips for peace of mind throughout the stay.</p>

<h2>National emergency numbers that apply in Skikda</h2>

<p>Algeria runs on a unified emergency number system across the whole country. There is no local number specific to the Skikda province: the three numbers below apply everywhere, from Filfila to the city centre, through Stora and Collo.</p>

<h3>Why three different numbers</h3>

<p>The split follows Algeria's territorial coverage logic. In urban areas and coastal municipalities like Filfila, national police and civil protection are the first point of contact. On roads connecting Skikda to surrounding towns or toward Collo and more rural areas, the national gendarmerie takes over. If in doubt about the right service, civil protection (14) remains the simplest reflex: it redirects to the right department if needed.</p>

<h2>Medical emergencies: the civil protection reflex</h2>

<p>For any medical emergency in Skikda, whether a sudden illness, a fall, a burn, or something more serious, <strong>14</strong> remains the central number. Civil protection handles emergency interventions and ambulance transport to the most suitable healthcare facility, day or night.</p>

<h3>In case of drowning or a sea incident</h3>

<p>On the province's busiest beaches, especially around Stora and Filfila during the swimming season (June to November), lifeguard presence is generally stronger during high season at the most supervised spots. In the event of an incident in the water, immediately alerting people nearby on the beach and dialling 14 without delay remains the priority reflex, even on a supervised beach.</p>

<h3>Pharmacies and minor issues</h3>

<p>For everyday needs (fever, digestive discomfort, minor injuries), a pharmacy is usually enough, with a duty rota system outside regular hours across the province's municipalities, including around Filfila. It's advisable to ask your host on arrival which duty pharmacy is active during your stay, as this schedule changes regularly from one establishment to the next.</p>

<h2>Useful numbers for international travellers</h2>

<p>Beyond the three national numbers, a few extra reflexes apply specifically to travellers from abroad, particularly diaspora families visiting from France or Europe.</p>

<ul>
  <li>Note your embassy or consulate's contact details in Algeria before departure, useful in case of a lost passport or administrative difficulty</li>
  <li>Check with your mobile carrier whether Algerian emergency numbers are reachable while roaming, or get a local SIM card on arrival</li>
  <li>Keep a digital copy and a separate paper copy of your passport and ID card</li>

</ul>

<h2>Road emergencies during an excursion</h2>

<p>Many visitors to Skikda use their stay to explore Collo, Cap Bougaroun, or the province's wooded hills. These roads, sometimes winding and less busy than the coastline, call for extra vigilance. In case of a breakdown, accident, or difficulty on a mountain road, the <strong>national gendarmerie (1055)</strong> is generally the most relevant contact, alongside civil protection for anyone injured.</p>

<h3>Before heading out on an excursion</h3>

<ul>
  <li>Check that your mobile plan gets proper signal outside urban areas before setting off on an isolated route</li>
  <li>Tell your host your planned route and estimated return time</li>
  <li>Pack water, a basic first-aid kit, and a portable phone charger</li>
  <li>Avoid, when possible, night driving on poorly lit mountain roads</li>
</ul>

<h2>Travel insurance and additional assistance</h2>

Taking out insurance before departure, checking the assistance number that comes with it, and saving it alongside the national numbers usefully rounds out your preparation, especially for travellers coming from Europe.</p>

<h2>What to actually do in an emergency</h2>

<p>How quickly and clearly you make the call matters as much as dialling the right number. A few simple reflexes help emergency services respond faster, especially for a visitor unfamiliar with local landmarks.</p>

<ol>
  <li>Stay calm and give the most precise location possible: municipality (Filfila, Skikda centre, Stora...), street name, or a visible landmark</li>
  <li>State the nature of the emergency in the first few words of the call</li>
  <li>Indicate how many people are involved and their apparent condition</li>
  <li>Stay reachable on the number used for the call, in case emergency services call back for directions</li>
  <li>Also notify your host, who knows the access points and can guide emergency services on site</li>
</ol>

<h3 class="p1">What are the emergency numbers to know in Skikda?</h3>
<p class="p2">Three national numbers cover most emergency situations across Algeria, including Skikda and Filfila: <strong>police on 17</strong>, <strong>civil protection on 14</strong> (fire, medical emergencies, accidents), and the <strong>national gendarmerie on 1055</strong>.
