<p>The <strong>Jeanne d'Arc district</strong> is a residential and seaside area of the Filfila municipality, in Skikda province, about 20 km from the city centre. It takes its name from the nearby beach, now officially renamed Larbi Ben M'hidi beach and about 7 km long, though the old toponym is still widely used in everyday speech. Résidence Oasis is located there, 700 m from the sea.</p>

<p>For a visitor preparing a stay in Skikda, this name keeps coming up in rental listings, WhatsApp exchanges with hosts, or directions given to a taxi driver, even though it no longer always appears on recent official maps. This article explains exactly where this area sits, what explains this dual naming, what you'll actually find there, and how to factor it into planning a stay in Filfila.</p>

<h2>Where exactly is the Jeanne d'Arc district</h2>

<p>The <strong>Jeanne d'Arc district</strong> belongs to the Filfila municipality, on the coastal front of Skikda province, east of the provincial capital. It sits about 20 km from downtown Skikda by road, in the same coastal stretch as Larbi Ben M'hidi beach and close to the Filfila nature reserve that borders this section of coastline.</p>

<h3>A landmark more than a strict administrative boundary</h3>

<p>In practice, "Jeanne d'Arc district" refers less to a precise administrative boundary than to an area recognised locally around the beach of the same original name. It's this landmark that residents, drivers and hosts use to orient a visitor, far more than the more recent official designations, which remain less known to the general public.</p>

<h2>Where does the name "Jeanne d'Arc" come from?</h2>

<p>Like many places on the Algerian coast, this part of Filfila carried a colonial-era name, that of the beach bordering it. Since independence, this beach has officially been named <strong>Larbi Ben M'hidi</strong>, in honour of a figure from the country's national history, as is the case for other renamed sites elsewhere in the country. The original name, however, lived on in everyday usage, to the point of remaining today the most common way to refer to the district among residents and visitors alike.</p>

<p>This coexistence of two names is not unusual in Algeria, where many places keep an inherited everyday name despite an official renaming. For a traveller, the practical point to remember is simple: searching for "Jeanne d'Arc" or "Larbi Ben M'hidi" generally leads to the same area of Filfila, and both names can be used interchangeably with local hosts or a taxi driver.</p>

<h2>Larbi Ben M'hidi beach, at the heart of the district</h2>

<p>The <strong>Larbi Ben M'hidi beach</strong>, formerly Jeanne d'Arc, stretches for about 7 km of sand, making it one of the longest shorelines in Skikda province. This length partly explains why the area attracts so many residences and seasonal rentals: the beach rarely feels crowded even in high season, unlike smaller coves elsewhere along the coast.</p>

<h3>A beach suited to daily use</h3>

<p>The swimming season runs from June to November, with water reaching around 26°C in August. The beach's length makes it easy to find a quieter spot, even in high season, just a few hundred metres from the busiest access points.</p>

<h2>What's in the district today</h2>

<p>The Jeanne d'Arc district mixes small residential buildings, furnished residences and a few local shops useful for everyday needs during a stay: grocery stores, a pharmacy and basic services. It remains overall more residential and family-oriented than the lively atmosphere of Stora's port or the large leisure complexes like Rusica Park, both a few kilometres away.</p>

<h3>A well-placed area relative to Filfila's attractions</h3>

<p>From the district, several sites in the area remain within easy reach: the Filfila nature reserve and its cork oak forest, the Marina d'Or water park, the Rusica Park complex, or Stora's fishing port for a boat trip. This central position makes it easy to alternate between the beach, short excursions and a quiet return home, without multiplying long drives.</p>

<h2>How to get there</h2>

<p>From downtown Skikda, allow about 20 km of road to reach the Jeanne d'Arc district in Filfila. No airport serves Skikda: the two closest airports are Constantine (Mohamed Boudiaf), about 100 km away, and Annaba (Rabah Bitat), about 110 km away. From either one, a private transfer booked in advance or a rental car remain the most reliable options to reach the area directly, especially with luggage or a tight arrival schedule.</p>

<h3>Getting around once there</h3>

<p>Once settled in the district, the nearby Filfila sites are easily reached by car or taxi over short distances. For occasional trips to Stora, downtown Skikda or Collo, renting a car for the day remains the most flexible option.</p>
