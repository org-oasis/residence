<p>Relaxing at a <strong>traditional hammam in Skikda</strong> is entirely possible: the town centre, about 20 km from Filfila, concentrates several establishments where steam bathing, exfoliation with a kessa glove and black soap are part of a ritual still practised daily. It's a cultural experience as much as a treatment, a natural addition to a beach stay in Filfila.</p>

<p>For a French-speaking traveller, an expat, or a member of the Maghrebi diaspora who knows the hammam through grandparents or stays in other North African countries, the question often comes up: where to find a reliable hammam near Skikda, how to prepare, and when in the stay to fit it in. This article covers the hammam tradition in the region, where to practise it, what to bring, and how to combine the experience with exploring the province's heritage.</p>

<h2>The hammam, a living tradition in Skikda</h2>

<p>The <strong>hammam</strong> remains a social institution as much as a body treatment in Algeria, and Skikda is no exception. Far from being an attraction reserved for tourists, it's a place visited daily by residents, where relaxation combines with a moment of exchange between relatives, neighbours or friends.</p>

<h3>A social ritual as much as a treatment</h3>

<p>Unlike a modern spa designed for passing visitors, the traditional hammam is part of a regular routine for many residents of the province. Visits are often organised in groups, among members of the same family or neighbourhood, which gives the place a convivial atmosphere quite different from a purely commercial establishment.</p>

<h3>Hammams for men and women: alternating time slots</h3>

<p>As in most traditional hammams across the Maghreb, access is generally organised into separate time slots for men and women, rather than a continuously mixed space. The exact hours vary from one establishment to another and are worth confirming on site or by phone before heading out.</p>

<h2>Where to go for a traditional hammam</h2>

<p>The distribution of the offer across Skikda province follows a classic pattern: the further you get from the urban centre, the fewer traditional establishments there are.</p>

<h3>Skikda town centre, the safe bet</h3>

<p>Skikda town centre, about 20 km from Filfila, remains the area where the most established and most frequented traditional hammams are concentrated. It's the most reliable destination for a full session, with a wider choice than elsewhere in the province.</p>

<h3>The rarer offer in Filfila</h3>

<p>Filfila, an essentially residential and beach district, doesn't have traditional establishments comparable to those in the town centre. For an authentic hammam experience, travelling to Skikda remains recommended rather than looking for an alternative directly in the Jeanne d'Arc district.</p>

<h2>What to bring for a hammam session</h2>

<p>Showing up prepared markedly changes the quality of the experience. A few accessories come up consistently in the practice of the traditional Maghrebi hammam.</p>

<ul>
  <li>An exfoliating glove (kessa), to remove dead skin once the pores have opened</li>
  <li>Traditional black soap, olive-oil based, applied before exfoliation</li>
  <li>Flip-flops or bath sandals, as the floor stays warm and damp throughout</li>
  <li>One or two towels, one to dry off, the other to sit on or cover up with</li>
  <li>Swimwear or underwear suited to the establishment's customs</li>
  <li>A bottle of water, as the heat of the rooms causes rapid dehydration</li>
</ul>

<h2>What a traditional hammam session involves</h2>

<p>A session generally follows a progression through rooms of increasing temperature, a principle inherited from Roman baths and present in most hammams across the Maghreb.</p>

<h3>The three classic stages</h3>

<p>The session starts in a warm room, meant to prepare the body without thermal shock. It continues in a hotter room, where sweating sets in and where exfoliation with the kessa glove generally takes place, revealing noticeably softer skin. The session ends with a rest period in a cooler area, often accompanied by mint tea to prolong the relaxation.</p>

<h3>Recommended duration and pace</h3>

<p>A full session generally lasts between one hour and an hour and a half, exfoliation and final rest included. Nothing requires sticking to a strict pace: it's entirely normal to extend the final rest, especially after a long beach day or a physical excursion.</p>

<h2>Good practice and etiquette at the hammam</h2>

<p>A few simple rules let you fully enjoy the hammam without feeling out of place, especially for a visitor experiencing it for the first time.</p>

<ul>
  <li>Respect the men's / women's time slots set by the establishment</li>
  <li>Avoid going right after a heavy meal, as the combined heat and digestion can cause discomfort</li>
  <li>Drink water regularly before, during and after the session</li>
  <li>Wear suitable bathing attire rather than being fully unclothed, unless the establishment says otherwise</li>
  <li>Stay alert to signs of fatigue or excessive heat, especially for a first hammam</li>
  <li>Allow a rest period after the session before resuming physical activity</li>
</ul>

<h2>When to enjoy a hammam during a stay in Skikda</h2>

<p>The hammam is practised year-round, but it takes on particular interest outside the bathing season, between October and May, when the weather limits long beach days. It's an activity that pairs well with a winter or low-season stay in Filfila. During the bathing season, from June to November, a late-afternoon hammam session also helps relax muscles after a swim or a hike, in a different register from the private jacuzzi offered in one of the residence's apartments.</p>

<h2>Common mistakes to avoid at the hammam</h2>

<p>A few mistakes recur among visitors experiencing the hammam for the first time, particularly if they're unfamiliar with local customs.</p>

<ul>
  <li>Showing up without checking the men's / women's time slot in effect beforehand</li>
  <li>Forgetting to bring your own kessa glove and black soap</li>
  <li>Going straight into a sea swim right after a hammam session, without a recovery period</li>
  <li>Underestimating the heat of the rooms for a first experience and staying too long without a break</li>
  <li>Planning the session late morning on a busy day without a time buffer</li>
</ul>
